# Malaysia Commends World Court For Recognising Rohingya, Says It Will Not Be Doing So Itself

KUALA LUMPUR: The Foreign Ministry on Thursday welcomed the International Court of Justice's judgment in The Gambia v. Myanmar as "a historic affirmation of the Rohingya people", while the Immigration Department confirmed that the affirmation does not extend to Malaysian territory.

The two statements were issued about three hours apart.

"Malaysia has consistently championed the plight of the Rohingya," the Foreign Ministry said. Officials later confirmed that the ministry had drafted two versions of the statement in advance, one for each possible outcome, and that both opened with that sentence.

"The plight of the Rohingya is too important to depend on the contents of a judgment," a ministry official said. "We were prepared to welcome it either way."

## A Consistent Position

Malaysia is not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention. Under the Immigration Act 1959/63, a person who has fled a massacre and a tourist who has overstayed a visa fall into the same legal category.

An Immigration Department spokesman said the department "fully respects" the court's findings.

"The court has recognised them, and we also recognise them," the spokesman said. "We recognise them as persons without valid travel documents. Our position is fully consistent with the judgment, which does not mention the Immigration Act."

Asked whether Rohingya holding UN refugee cards would be treated differently after the ruling, the spokesman said the cards would continue to receive "due consideration" at each officer's discretion.

"Discretion is very important," he said. "Otherwise it would be a law."

The Registrar of Societies said applications to register Rohingya associations would continue to be assessed in line with established practice.

"We follow precedent," a registry official said. "The precedent is that we have never approved one."

Asked how a precedent for approval could be established, the official said it would require an earlier approval.

## Regional Response

Malaysia said it would raise the judgment at ASEAN, where joint positions are adopted by consensus of all 11 member states, including Myanmar.

"We are confident of reaching consensus," the Foreign Ministry official said, "on the need for further consultations."

ASEAN adopted a Five-Point Consensus on Myanmar in April 2021. Diplomats said the bloc remained fully committed to it and described its implementation as "ongoing".

Myanmar's government, which does not use the term Rohingya, said it was unable to comment on a ruling concerning a group it does not recognise.

## Community Reaction

A 31-year-old construction worker who holds a UN refugee card followed the reading of the judgment on his phone during his lunch break. He asked not to be named because he works without a permit, which is the only way he is able to work.

"It is good that the court says what happened," he said. "I hope the officer at the roadblock near the site was also watching."

Asked whether Malaysia would now consider acceding to the Refugee Convention, the Foreign Ministry official said the matter remained under study.

He declined to say when the study began, saying that information was also under study.
