# Malaysia Thanks EU For Collecting Its Carbon Tax

KUALA LUMPUR: The government on Tuesday welcomed the European Union's carbon border levy as "an efficient collection partnership", saying the arrangement allows Malaysia to keep its own carbon tax deferred while ensuring that emissions from Malaysian steel are still priced, invoiced and paid in full.

The payments are made in euros, to Brussels.

Under the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, which entered its definitive phase on 1 January, European importers of steel, aluminium and other goods must account for the emissions released in making them. A carbon price already paid in the country of origin can be deducted from the bill.

"Because no carbon price is paid in Malaysia, nothing is deducted," said a spokesman for the Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability Ministry. "This makes the calculation very straightforward for everyone. Simplicity is one of our guiding principles."

The government deferred the carbon tax on 5 October, citing geopolitical tensions and volatile energy prices. Officials said on Tuesday that the tax would be introduced once global uncertainty falls to an acceptable level, to be set by a technical committee whose first task will be to decide what the committee should set.

"We cannot put the cart before the horse," the spokesman said. "The horse is currently under review."

## Sequencing

According to the ministry, the carbon tax will follow a Climate Change Bill, which will follow a regulatory framework, which will follow consultations on the framework's scope. A National Carbon Market Policy was launched in April and remains, officials said, "fully launched".

Asked whether exporters would be better off paying a carbon price at home, where the revenue would stay in the country, the spokesman said the government was "studying all options, including that one".

The Finance Ministry confirmed that carbon tax collections for 2026 had met projections exactly.

"We projected zero, and we collected zero," a ministry official said. "Not every revenue line can say that."

## Industry Relief

The steel industry welcomed the deferral, which it said would shield members from additional costs during a difficult period.

"We are very grateful that we will not be paying a carbon price in Malaysia," said a representative of a steel industry association, speaking after a briefing on the emissions paperwork now required by European buyers. "Our members are preparing to pay a carbon price in Europe."

Asked to explain the difference, the representative said: "The Malaysian one could be postponed."

Industry groups have asked Putrajaya to seek "flexibility" from Brussels. An EU trade official said the Union remained open to dialogue.

"We are very happy to deduct a carbon price paid at home," the official said. "We are equally happy not to." The official stressed that the levy "is not a revenue measure", adding that revenue projections had been "encouraging".

## Climate Talks

At the UN climate summit in Turkey this week, the Malaysian delegation presented the deferral as evidence of a "measured and pragmatic" approach.

"Malaysia remains committed to net zero as early as 2050," the delegation's statement read, "and to a carbon tax as late as necessary."

An economist who studies carbon pricing at a Malaysian university said the deferral had not removed the cost to exporters.

"It removed the receipt," she said.

## Local Delivery

Residents living beside steel mills said they had not been consulted on the deferral, but confirmed that the mills' emissions were still being delivered locally, at no charge.

"We are not exporters," said a retired schoolteacher whose house faces a mill chimney. "We just live here."

At press time, the ministry said it was assessing whether residents' exposure could be counted toward Malaysia's carbon price as "a contribution in kind".
