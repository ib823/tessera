# BRIEF: Subsidy rationalisation savings cited at about RM15.5 billion a year; no independent delivery audit

**Slug:** subsidy-rationalisation-targets-rm8-billion-annual
**Issue ID:** 1102
**Source date:** 2026-04-08
**Backfill date: 2026-11-08**
**Brief status:** Source-list backfill. Issue cards unchanged. (The slug reflects an earlier edition's RM8 billion headline; the current cards use RM15.5 billion.)

## ISSUE

In the Budget 2026 speech (10 October 2025), Prime Minister and Finance Minister Anwar Ibrahim said targeted subsidies had freed up an estimated RM15.5 billion a year, led by electricity tariff restructuring (about RM6 billion on average) and targeted diesel (RM5 billion), with RON95 targeting (BUDI95, from late September 2025) expected to save at least RM2.5 billion. PADU, the central database with about 30.4 million individual profiles, is MOF's reference point for identifying RON95 recipients. The issue argues that the savings are documented by the implementing ministries themselves, and that the next test is independent, per-cohort measurement of targeting accuracy.

## CONTRADICTIONS

- None between primary sources on the cards' figures. Note on framing: the RM15.5 billion is a ministry estimate that mixes realised savings (diesel, electricity, chicken, eggs) with a component described as potential (RON95, "at least 2.5 billion") [1]. The card's "savings ... to date" wording is broadly fair, since diesel and electricity make up the bulk.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- **Fuel subsidy bill surged in 2026.** Fiscal Outlook 2027 and the Budget 2027 speech report that the West Asia crisis raised fuel subsidy spending to RM40 billion in 2026 and that fuel subsidies are expected to remain about RM40 billion in 2027 [7, 8]. The BUDI95 quota was cut temporarily to 200 litres from 1 Apr 2026 and restored to 300 litres from 1 Sep 2026 [7, 9]. Budget 2027 argues that, without targeting, "the fiscal burden would have exceeded 40 billion ringgit" [8]. The cards' RM15.5 billion savings frame now sits beside a much larger gross subsidy bill; a reader could take "savings" as a falling bill, which it was not in 2026.
- **New usage data.** Budget 2027 says only about 100,000 users (under 1%) reached the 300-litre BUDI95 limit in September 2026 [8]. This is a ministry figure, not an independent audit.
