## ISSUE

Between 21 August and 3 September 2026, smoke from fires in Indonesian Kalimantan pushed Sarawak's
Air Pollutant Index (API) into the hazardous band for the longest stretch since 2015. Serian peaked
at **475** on 31 August. The state closed **591 schools** (197,323 pupils) on 21 August, cancelled
its outdoor Merdeka celebration, cancelled flights out of Kuching, and set a localised emergency
trigger at API 500. The Health Ministry recorded a **124.4 % week-on-week rise** in upper respiratory
tract infections. ASEAN activated its highest transboundary-haze alert, Level 3, on 27 August.

Mainstream coverage carries the daily API readings, the school closures, the cloud seeding, and the
diplomatic letters at volume. What it does not carry is the **legislative record**:

- Malaysia proposed its own Transboundary Haze Pollution Act in September 2019.
- The Pakatan Harapan Cabinet agreed to it **in principle on 12 February 2020**.
- The Perikatan Nasional government **shelved it in August 2020** ("enhance cooperation with
  neighbouring countries and ASEAN").
- The Pakatan Harapan-led government **dropped it for good on 6 November 2023**, in a written
  parliamentary reply, after advice from the Attorney General's Chambers that prosecution would need
  evidence (maps, coordinates, land ownership) the state of origin would not hand over.
- The government said in December 2023 that a policy paper would go to a parliamentary select
  committee in the first half of 2024. No outcome has been published.
- Suhakam recommended a Clean Air Act covering transboundary pollution in September 2024. One of its
  six recommendations has been implemented, and it is not that one.

The developable core is not "Indonesia is burning" (Indonesia's own ministry has 335 concession
companies with hotspots inside their licences and has arrested 72 people). It is that **both
Malaysian coalitions had the same law on the table and both chose diplomacy over statute**, and
that the emergency threshold the state is now watching, API 500, is an administrative trigger last
fired in 2013, not a health standard. Meanwhile the only country in ASEAN with such a law, Singapore,
has never secured a conviction under it. That is the honest tension the issue must hold: the law
was dropped for reasons that are real, and the alternative has produced the worst season in eleven
years.

Anger-at-process, not anger-at-Indonesia and not anger-at-any-minister: the record is bipartisan.

## RELEVANT LAW

- **Environmental Quality Act 1974 (Act 127), s.29A** — open-burning prohibition, inserted after the 1997–98 haze. Territorial; does not reach fires abroad. [S17]
- **ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution (2002)** — "Done at Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia on the tenth day of June in the year two thousand and two." Art. 3(1): parties have "the responsibility to ensure that activities within their jurisdiction or control do not cause damage to the environment and harm to human health of other States". Art. 4(1)–(2): co-operate on prevention and monitoring; "when the transboundary haze pollution originates from within their territories, respond promptly to a request for relevant information or consultations". Art. 9: each party shall undertake measures to prevent and control fire-related activities, the first of which is **"developing and implementing legislative and other regulatory measures, as well as programmes and strategies to promote zero burning policy"** (wording per [S14b] and [S54]; seven sub-paragraphs in total). Art. 27: disputes "shall be settled amicably by consultation or negotiation". **No article provides penalties or sanctions for non-compliance.** [S14], [S14b] — GAP 1 closed on the treaty text as mirrored by the University of Oslo law library; the asean.org PDF itself could not be text-extracted in session, so re-check the Art. 9(a) phrase against it at Phase 6.
- **Singapore Transboundary Haze Pollution Act 2014** — offence of causing or contributing to haze pollution in Singapore by conduct outside Singapore; presumptions of ownership; fines up to S$100,000 per day, max S$2 million. [S16]
- **National Haze Action Plan / NADMA directive** — disaster committee activation at API >150 for 24 h; school closures at API >200; cloud seeding at >150 for 24 h; emergency declaration at API ≥500 with closure of government and private premises except essential services. [S5], [S9], [S41]
- **Malaysia Ambient Air Quality Standard (DOE)** — PM2.5 24-hour limit 35 µg/m³ from 2020 (IT-1 75 in 2015; IT-2 50 in 2018); WHO 2021 guideline 15 µg/m³. API for PM2.5 is a 24-hour moving average. [S42], [S43]
- **Federal Constitution** — no explicit environmental right; Suhakam's 2024 report recommends recognising one. [S3]

## RECOMMENDED LENSES

1. **Environmental** — the numbers: 475, 591 schools, 1,180 hotspots, +86 %. Restores a lens the 2000-series has never carried.
2. **Legal** — the four-date record: 12 Feb 2020 in principle → 3 Aug 2020 shelved → 6 Nov 2023 dropped → Sep 2024 Suhakam. Plus the honest counter: AGC's evidence problem, Singapore's zero convictions.
3. **Regional** — Indonesia's side of the ledger: 335 companies, 72 arrests, six sanctions, a BNPB budget at a 15-year low, and Dasopang's "you have plantations here too" with the 2019 KLK/Sime/IOI/TDM precedent.
   (Alternative third lens: **Health**, using KKM's ME32→ME33 doubling.)

Reframe candidate (value restructuring): *The API-500 emergency line is not a health standard. It is an administrative trigger that has fired three times in 29 years, and it sits at fourteen times the daily limit Malaysia set for itself in 2020.* (Check arithmetic against the API/PM2.5 breakpoint before use: API 500 ≈ PM2.5 500 µg/m³ 24 h vs MAAQS 35 µg/m³ — GAP 4.)

Reframe candidate (causal): *Haze is not weather. Two governments held the same bill and both filed it under diplomacy.*

## CONTRADICTIONS

1. **Indonesian July 2026 burn area.** Al Jazeera: "nearly 95,000 ha" in July [S46b]; Mongabay citing BNPB: 124,040 ha in July [S47]; AP: ~182,000 ha in July, 285,000 ha for the year [S46]. Different agencies (BNPB vs Forestry Ministry vs KLH) and different cut-off dates. **Use only the year-to-date figure with attribution ("Indonesian officials put the area burned this year at about 285,000 ha"), or drop.** Do not put a July figure on a card.
2. **Malaysian asthma numbers.** KKM (27 Aug): asthma 61 → 158 cases ME32→ME33 [S7]. Al Jazeera (1 Sep) attributes to Malaysia's Health Ministry "1,811 asthma cases in the week of 23–29 August, up from 219" [S46b]. Different weeks, but a tenfold difference in scale that KKM's own statement does not support. **Use only the KKM 27 August figures until KKM's ME34/ME35 statement is located (GAP 3).** Sarawak's deputy minister separately says statewide asthma "remained generally stable" [S12].
3. **2013 haze cost.** MJPHM review citing Shahwahid 2016: RM1.74 bn total [S49]; Malay Mail 2019 headline citing a UKM study: RM1.57 bn [S50, not opened]. Use RM1.74 bn with the itemised breakdown from the peer-reviewed source, or Yeo's "more than RM1 billion" as a floor.
4. **AATHP entry into force.** ECOLEX: 10 November 2003 [S14]; other secondary sources give 25 November 2003. Write "November 2003".
5. **2019 sealed plantations.** Indonesia named four Malaysian-linked units [S22]; Sime Darby and IOI said no sealing had taken place as of 13 Sep 2019; KLK confirmed a fire on PT Adei land [S23]. Report the naming and the denials together, or use only KLK's confirmed case.
6. **Who pays for the 2026 fires.** Marwan Dasopang says Malaysian and Singaporean plantations "also have a role" [S34]; no Indonesian ministry statement found naming any Malaysian-linked company in 2026. **Do not imply Malaysian companies caused the 2026 fires.** The 2019 precedent is the only evidence that the question is live.
