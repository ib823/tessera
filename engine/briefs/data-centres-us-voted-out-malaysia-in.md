## ISSUE

**Thesis under test.** Three things happened within four days:

- **3 November 2026.** US voters favoured limiting data-centre development in 40 of 41 local ballot
  measures across 10 states [S30].
- **4 November.** Google committed a further US$15 billion to Malaysia for 2027–2028. The money goes
  to campuses in **Selangor and Negeri Sembilan, not Johor** [S1].
- **6 November.** Protesters broke into a Microsoft data-centre construction site in Amsterdam
  [S34, S35].

Malaysia has written cost-causation rules for data centres. Ministers' written parliamentary replies
list four:

- an Ultra High Voltage (UHV) tariff since 1 July 2025;
- a higher Connected Load Charge;
- payment for contracted capacity even when it goes unused;
- a separate data-centre water tariff, plus capital contributions to water infrastructure
  [S11, S12, S13, S28].

The decision process gives residents little *formal* voice:

- Data centres are not a prescribed activity under the EIA Order 2015. The nearest trigger
  ("industrial estate" of 20 ha or more) sits in the First Schedule, which carries no public display
  or comment unless the Director General orders it [S2].
- Approvals run through MIDA, the Data Centre Task Force (DCTF) and, in Johor, a state technical
  committee with three exco members [S15, S24].
- The national planning guideline sets application tracks of 99, 29 and 22 days and contains no
  objection step [S4].
- The Digital Infrastructure (Data Centre) Bill is not due at Cabinet until 2027 [S17].

Meanwhile the scale keeps rising:

- 117 data centres are approved, with about 23,500MW of maximum demand on completion (Johari, MITI)
  [S18].
- Approved grid supply stood at 12.3GW in August (Fadillah, PETRA) [S11].
- BMI projects Johor's data centres could take **more than 90% of new Peninsular generation** over
  five years [S42].

**What the brief must keep prominent, so the issue cannot be read as anti-investment:**

1. **Counter-evidence on voice.** TCPA 1976 s21(6) gives neighbouring landowners a 21-day right to
   object, with a hearing under s21(7). That right applies only where **no local plan exists** [S3].
   Even so, in Kota Damansara a residents' coalition of more than 500 households objected and the
   developer withdrew its planning application on 28 July 2026 [S27].
2. **Google's own commitments.** Google says it will pay 100% of its campuses' power and the direct
   costs of connecting them to the grid [S1]. It will also build a 65 MLD reservoir and treatment
   plant, whose ownership passes to the Negeri Sembilan government [S21, S23].

**Developable core.** The angle is anger at process: the order in which Malaysia built its data-centre
rules. Do not aim it at Google, at investors, or at Johor's government. The bill rules came first and
are written into tariffs and gazettes. The voice rules are partial: a neighbour-notice clause that
switches off where a local plan exists, an EIA list with no data-centre line, and a Bill dated 2027.

Wording discipline throughout:

- US measures: "favoured limiting" or "voted to limit", never "rejected".
- Malaysian households: no claim that they currently subsidise data centres. The government says the
  opposite, and this brief found no evidence either way on RP4 capex recovery (GAP 2).

## RELEVANT LAW

- **Environmental Quality Act 1974 (Act 127), s34A, and the EIA Order 2015, P.U.(A) 195/2015** [S2]:
  - Para 3(1): First and Second Schedule activities are prescribed activities.
  - Para 3(2): First Schedule activities "do not require public display and public comment unless
    otherwise instructed, in writing, by the Director General".
  - Para 3(3): Second Schedule activities "require public display and public comment".
  - **No data-centre entry.** The nearest triggers are First Schedule "Development of industrial
    estate covering an area of 20 hectares or more" and, for on-site generation, power-station
    thresholds (e.g. fossil-fuel steam plant of 10MW or more).
  - Offence, fine and prison figures for operating without approval are reported by Rajah & Tann
    [S47]; the penalty section itself was not opened (GAP 9).
- **Town and Country Planning Act 1976 (Act 172), s21(6)–(8)** (reprint as at 1 June 2021) [S3]:
  - (6) "If the proposed development is located in an area in respect of which no local plan exists
    for the time being … the local planning authority shall, by notice in writing served on the owners
    of the neighbouring lands inform them of their right to object … within twenty-one days".
  - (7) A hearing follows within 30 days if requested.
  - (8) "Neighbouring lands" means adjoining lots, lots across a road of 20m or less, or lots within
    200m on a shared cul-de-sac.
  - **Scope limit:** the right applies only where no local plan exists, and only to landowners.
- **Garis Panduan Perancangan Pusat Data (PLANMalaysia, first printing 2024; Cabinet 8 Oct 2024)**
  [S4]:
  - Must be read with Act 172.
  - Lists DOE's role as "Penilaian Kesan Kepada Alam Sekeliling" (EIA).
  - Buffer: "Perlu disediakan 50m zon penampan … dengan sempadan lot perumahan; atau • Tidak perlu
    zon penampan sekiranya tahap kebisingan tidak > 5dB daripada tahap kebisingan asal." For
    conversions of existing buildings the buffer is set "case by case".
  - The Industrial Green Lane track lists, among its criteria, that the proposal "telah memperolehi
    kelulusan Laporan EIA, TIA dan RSA" (has obtained EIA, TIA and RSA approval). The text does not
    say whether that EIA is the estate's or the data centre's.
  - Guideline, not statute. Legal weight not established (GAP 8).
- **Water Services Industry Act 2006 (Act 655), ss84(2)(a), 179(i), and state rate regulations:**
  - Kelantan P.U.(A) 226/2025 defines "Data centre" ("Premises specifically used to centrally house
    equipment and infrastructure of information technology and communication …") at a flat RM5.32/m³,
    minimum RM53.20 a month, from 1 Aug 2025 [S5].
  - Fadillah reports RM5.30–5.38/m³ across the Peninsula and Labuan [S11].
  - The Johor regulation was not opened (GAP 6).
- **Electricity tariffs (RP4, from 1 Jul 2025):**
  - myTNB lists the "Ultra High Voltage Tariff" and "Bulk Ultra High Voltage Tariff" as new categories
    [S6].
  - Knight Frank reports UHV time-of-use energy charges of 51.09–55.18 sen/kWh plus capacity and
    network charges [S40].
  - **The ST tariff schedule was not opened (GAP 1).** Connected Load Charge and pay-for-contracted
    capacity rest on ministers' replies via news [S11, S28].
- **Planned: Digital Infrastructure (Data Centre) Bill.** Due at Cabinet in 2027. No draft or
  consultation found [S17].
- **Comparators (opened):**
  - Ireland CRU LEU decision CRU/2025236 (12 Dec 2025): direction under s34 of the Electricity
    Regulation Act 1999 [S9]. Thresholds per Matheson: 1–<10 MVA needs on-site generation equal to
    100% of maximum import capacity; 10 MVA and above needs dispatchable on-site or proximate
    generation or storage; 80% of annual demand matched with new Irish renewables [S50].
  - Singapore DC-CFA2 (200MW, Jurong Island, more than 50% green energy) [S8].

## RECOMMENDED LENSES

1. **Governance:** who decides, in what order.
   - No data-centre line in the EIA Order. The nearest trigger has no public display.
   - The TCPA neighbour notice switches off wherever a local plan exists.
   - The national guideline's 99, 29 and 22-day tracks have no objection step.
   - DCTF and Johor's three-exco committee decide behind closed doors.
   - The Bill is due at Cabinet in 2027.

## WHAT WEAKENS THE THESIS

- **The US votes were narrower than "rejection":**
  - Of the 27 limiting measures approved, 12 were size-threshold bans, 3 were six-month moratoriums
    and 2 were non-binding [S30].
  - 7 of the 13 defeated pro-development measures were non-binding [S30].
  - Harwood's $3b campus proceeds [S31].
  - Emporia's ban awaits a court ruling on the petition's validity (reported; not opened).
- **The US build-out is not being pushed out:** AP cites PwC's forecast of US$15.1 trillion in US
  data-centre capex for 2026–2050 vs US$5.6 trillion in Europe [S34]. The pushback abroad is also
  years old: Amsterdam 2019, Singapore 2019–2022, Dublin 2021 [S40, S7].
- **Malaysia's cost-causation rules are on paper and specific:**
  - UHV tariff; higher Connected Load Charge.
  - Pay for full contracted capacity ("300MW … uses only 100MW … must still pay for the full 300MW")
    [S28].
  - Gazetted data-centre water tariff [S5]; developer capital for water infrastructure [S13].
  - 30% renewables [S11]; WUE 2.2 [S13].
- **Google's own terms:** pays 100% of power and direct grid costs; 65 MLD plant to the state; water
  cooling only where its risk assessment found watersheds "not at high risk"; PUE 1.12–1.14 [S1].
  **No Google site is in Johor.**
- **Johor screens and refuses:** about 30% of applications rejected Jun–Oct 2024 [S7]; no Tier 1 or 2
  data centres [S26]; a three-exco technical committee [S24]; recycled and river-water plants now
  running [S26, S25].
- **Residents can and did stop a project:** Kota Damansara [S27]. The Gelang Patah protest leader now
  says he is "not worried about the water" [S26].
- **Parliament scrutinises:** written replies to MPs across the benches [S11, S13, S14]; NEAC has
  committed to a Bill [S17].
- **Financial risk is small so far:** about 1% of bank loans [S44]; AMRO puts the reserve margin at
  28–36% for 2024–2030 and finds Malaysia among the lowest in water stress regionally [S7]. BNM's
  FSR 2H2025 mentions data centres only as construction activity [S10].

**What still supports the core (keep alongside):**

- No data-centre EIA category; the nearest trigger has no public comment [S2].
- The TCPA notice is limited to areas without a local plan [S3].
- The national guideline shows no objection step in the text extracted [S4].
- Approvals run through the DCTF and state committees [S15, S24].
- Public money funds Johor reservoirs: RM5b [S26].
- BMI's 90% projection [S42]; utilisation of 54–58.5% leaves room for stranded capacity, which the
  Energy Commission itself flagged when it revised projections [S46].
- Household bill protection expires 31 Dec 2026 [S19].
- Ireland consulted for two and a half years and then required data centres to bring their own
  dispatchable generation; Malaysia "encourages" self-generation [S9, S11].
