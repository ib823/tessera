## ISSUE

**Global news peg, not cause.** Within four days:

- **3 Nov 2026.** Voters in 41 local US ballot contests, across 10 states and 21 of them in Ohio,
  favoured limiting data-centre development in 40 (Ballotpedia; results unofficial) [S29, S30].
- **4 Nov.** Google committed a further US$15 billion to Malaysia for 2027–2028 (RM61b at Bernama's
  US$1 = RM4.07), for campuses in **Selangor and Negeri Sembilan, not Johor** [S1, S20].
- **6 Nov.** Protesters broke into a Microsoft data-centre construction site in Amsterdam [S34, S35].
- **October.** A Finnish authority halted two Google sites pending mandatory EIAs [S39].

No source links Google's Malaysia plan to the US votes. The plan builds on a 2024 investment [S1], and
AP cites a forecast of US$15.1 trillion in US data-centre capex for 2026–2050 [S34]. The peg shows how
other places put data centres to the public. It is not evidence that data centres are moving here.

**Thesis (narrowed after verification): an asymmetry of specificity, not of sequence.**

**(1) Malaysia has written data-centre-specific rules on who pays.**
- A gazetted data-centre water tariff from 1 Aug 2025. The Kelantan gazette sets a flat RM5.32/m³
  [S5]; Fadillah gives RM5.30–5.38/m³ for the Peninsula and Labuan [S11].
- Developers contribute capital for water treatment and distribution [S13].
- Data centres must source at least 30% of their energy from renewables [S11].
- Two general large-user rules ministers cite for data centres in written replies:
  - an Ultra High Voltage (UHV) tariff since 1 Jul 2025, covering all supply above 50kV, data centres
    included;
  - a higher Connected Load Charge [S12, S13].
- Trade press reports that operators must pay for their full contracted capacity even when it goes
  unused, "as announced in Parliament earlier" [S28]. That rule is not traced to a named written reply
  (GAP 3a).

**(2) Malaysia has written no data-centre-specific rule on who is heard. The general rules apply,
with these limits:**
- **Plan stage.** Residents may object to a draft local plan or its alteration. The council must
  advertise for at least four weeks in three issues of two local newspapers. A local inquiry is at the
  council's discretion (TCPA ss12A, 13, 14, 16(3)) [S3]. Permission may not contravene the plan
  (s22(4)(a)) [S3].
- **Project stage.** Neighbouring landowners get a 21-day right to object, and a hearing if requested,
  **only where no local plan exists** (s21(6)–(7)) [S3]. Planning permission is the local council's
  decision (s21(1)) [S3].
- **Environmental stage.** The EIA Order 2015 has no data-centre entry. The nearest trigger, an
  industrial estate of 20 ha or more, sits in the First Schedule: no public display or comment unless
  the Director General directs it [S2].
- **Data-centre planning guideline (2024).** It sets 99-, 29-, 22- and 33-day tracks and shows no
  objection step in the text extracted [S4]. The 99- and 29-day tracks presuppose conforming land
  (consistency with land conditions and an approved layout or master plan) [S4].
- **Utility screening.** The Data Centre Task Force (DCTF; "relevant ministries and agencies")
  assesses power and water [S15]. In Johor, a technical committee evaluates data-centre investments,
  and three exco members also review entries [S24].
- **Coming legislation.** The Digital Infrastructure (Data Centre) Bill is due at Cabinet in 2027. No
  draft or consultation has been published [S17].

**Scale (with caveats attached):**
- Johari (MITI) says 117 data centres are approved, with about 23,500MW of maximum demand on
  completion [S18]. Many of the 27 earliest-stage projects expect off-grid supply [S28].
- Approved grid supply stood at 12.3GW in August [S11].
- BMI projects Johor's data centres could take more than 90% of the Peninsula's additional generation
  over five years. The same note says generation is expected to meet demand in its base case [S42].

**Kept prominent so the issue cannot be read as anti-investment:**
1. **Kota Damansara (Selangor).** A coalition of more than 500 households objected. MBPJ extended the
   objection window. On 28 Jul 2026 the consultant withdrew the planning application "to re-evaluate
   the feasibility of the site" (MBPJ) [S27]. This is the strongest counter-evidence that local voice
   operates. No Johor case shows a resident objection changing an outcome.
2. **Google's own commitments** [S1]:
   - pays 100% of its campuses' power and the direct costs of connecting them to the grid;
   - from 2027, plans programmes to bring down household electricity bills in host states;
   - river-basin replenishment in the Sungai Buloh and Sungai Selangor basins;
   - discloses water use and PUE per site.
   - The Prime Minister said a 65 MLD reservoir and treatment plant will pass to the state as a public
     asset [S21, S23]. W.Media reports it serves both Google's Port Dickson campus and public use [S45].

**Developable core.** The angle is anger at process, the asymmetry between the two halves. Do not aim
it at Google, at investors, at Johor, or at the US. The cost rules are data-centre-specific and written
into a tariff gazette and ministers' replies. The voice rules are general: a plan-stage objection
window, a project-level notice that applies only where no local plan exists, an EIA list with no
data-centre line, and a Bill due at Cabinet in 2027. **Do not claim the cost rules "came first"**: TCPA
s21(6), the EIA Order and the planning guideline all predate the UHV tariff (C1).

**Wording discipline:**
- US measures: "favoured limiting" or "voted to limit", never "rejected"; "partial bans", not
  "size-threshold bans".
- Households: no claim that they subsidise data centres. The government states the intent to shield
  them, and RP4 recovery is untraced (GAP 2).

## RELEVANT LAW

- **Town and Country Planning Act 1976 (Act 172), reprint as at 1 June 2021** [S3]:
  - **Plan-stage objection (all planned areas):**
    - s12A: publicity before preparing a draft local plan, and an opportunity for representations.
    - s13(2): notice "in three issues of at least two local newspapers", with a period of "not less
      than four weeks" for "objections to or representations in respect of the draft local plan";
      extendable once by up to four weeks (s13(3)).
    - s14(1): the council "may cause a local inquiry or other hearing to be held" (discretionary).
    - s16(3): the same steps apply to alteration, revocation or replacement of a local plan.
  - **s21(1):** an application for planning permission "shall be made to the local planning
    authority".
  - **s21(6) project-stage notice:** "If the proposed development is located in an area in respect of
    which no local plan exists for the time being … the local planning authority shall, by notice in
    writing served on the owners of the neighbouring lands inform them of their right to object …
    within twenty-one days".
    - (7) hearing within 30 days if requested.
    - (8) "neighbouring lands" means adjoining lots, lots across a road, lane, drain or reserve of
      20m or less, or lots within 200m on a shared cul-de-sac.
    - **Scope:** applies only where no local plan exists, and only to landowners.
  - **s22(4)(a):** the council "shall not grant planning permission if … the development … would
    contravene any provision of the development plan".
  - Net effect: in planned areas the public's formal objection point is the zoning plan, not the
    individual project.
- **Environmental Quality Act 1974 (Act 127), s34A; EIA Order 2015, P.U.(A) 195/2015** [S2]:
  - Para 3(2): First Schedule activities "do not require public display and public comment unless
    otherwise instructed, in writing, by the Director General".
  - Para 3(3): Second Schedule activities "require public display and public comment". The Order
    provides no hearing for any activity.
  - **No data-centre, computer or ICT entry in either Schedule.**
  - Nearest triggers:
    - First Schedule: industrial estate of 20 ha or more; fossil-fuel (non-coal) steam power station
      of 10MW or more.
    - Second Schedule: coal-fired power station of 10MW or more; nuclear power station.
  - Penalty figures (RM100,000–RM1m, up to five years) come from Rajah & Tann [S47]; the statute's
    text was not opened (GAP 9).
- **Garis Panduan Perancangan Pusat Data (PLANMalaysia, first printing 2024; Cabinet 8 Oct 2024)**
  [S4]:
  - Read with Act 172; lists DOE's role as "Penilaian Kesan Kepada Alam Sekeliling".
  - Buffer: "Perlu disediakan 50m zon penampan … dengan sempadan lot perumahan; atau • Tidak perlu
    zon penampan sekiranya tahap kebisingan tidak > 5dB daripada tahap kebisingan asal"; conversions
    "case by case".
  - Tracks:
    - Kaedah 1 (99 days) requires consistency with express land conditions and an existing layout
      approval.
    - The Industrial Green Lane (29 days) applies only in planned industrial areas with an approved
      master-plan layout, and lists EIA, TIA and RSA approval among its criteria.
  - No objection step in the text extracted. Legal weight not established (GAP 8).
- **Water Services Industry Act 2006 (Act 655), ss84(2)(a), 179(i); state rate regulations:**
  - Kelantan P.U.(A) 226/2025 defines "Data centre" at a flat RM5.32/m³ (minimum RM53.20 a month), in
    force 1 Aug 2025 [S5].
  - Peninsula and Labuan range RM5.30–5.38/m³ (Fadillah) [S11].
  - Johor's regulation was not opened (GAP 6).
- **Electricity tariffs (RP4, from 1 Jul 2025):**
  - The UHV category covers all large consumers supplied above 50kV, data centres included [S12, S13].
    myTNB shows the category exists, without rates or dates [S6].
  - Knight Frank's illustrative 51.09–55.18 sen/kWh energy charge assumes full-load 24/7 [S40].
  - ST schedule not opened (GAP 1). The pay-for-contracted-capacity rule is reported by W.Media only
    (GAP 3a).
- **Planned:** Digital Infrastructure (Data Centre) Bill, due at Cabinet in 2027; no draft [S17].
- **Comparators (opened):**
  - Ireland CRU decision CRU2025236 (12 Dec 2025), a direction under s34 of the Electricity Regulation
    Act 1999 [S9]. Per Matheson: on-site generation for 1–<10 MVA; dispatchable on-site or proximate
    generation or storage at least equal to the connection for 10 MVA and above; 80% renewable
    matching [S50].
  - Singapore DC-CFA2, 200MW provisional [S8].

## WHAT WEAKENS THE THESIS

- **The peg is narrower than "rejection":**
  - 41 local contests, 21 in Ohio.
  - 12 of the 27 approved limits are **partial bans**; 3 are six-month moratoriums; 2 are non-binding.
  - 7 of the 13 defeated pro-development measures were non-binding.
  - Results are unofficial [S29, S30].
  - Harwood's $3b campus proceeds [S31].
- **No displacement evidence.** Google's plan builds on its 2024 investment [S1]. AP cites US$15.1tn of
  forecast US data-centre capex for 2026–2050 vs US$5.6tn in Europe [S34]. Pushback abroad is years
  old: Amsterdam 2019 and 2022, Singapore 2019–2022, Dublin 2021 [S40, S7].
- **Cost rules are specific:**
  - Gazetted data-centre water tariff [S5]; developer capital for water [S13]; 30% renewables [S11];
    WUE 2.2 [S13].
  - UHV and a higher Connected Load Charge apply to data centres among large users [S12, S13].
  - Contracted-capacity payment: "300MW … uses only 100MW … must still pay for the full 300MW"
    (W.Media) [S28].
- **Google's own terms** [S1]:
  - pays 100% of power and direct grid-connection costs; 2027 programmes to bring down household bills
    in host states;
  - river-basin replenishment; per-site water and PUE disclosure;
  - water cooling only where its assessment found watersheds "not at high risk"; PUE 1.12–1.14;
  - 65 MLD plant to the state (PM) [S21].
  - **No Google site is in Johor.**
- **The voice is not zero:**
  - Zoning is open to public objection for at least four weeks, with newspaper notice (TCPA ss13,
    16(3)) [S3].
  - Permission may not contravene the plan (s22(4)(a)) [S3].
  - Kota Damansara residents objected; the consultant later withdrew, citing a feasibility review [S27].
  - Johor's technical committee and exco members review entries [S24].
  - About 30% of Johor applications were rejected in Jun–Oct 2024 [S7]; no Tier 1 or 2 [S26].
- **Parliament scrutinises:** questions from PN-Besut, PN-Machang, PH-Bukit Bendera and the Batu Pahat
  MP [S16, S13, S11, S14]; NEAC committed to a Bill [S17].
- **Financial risk looks small so far:** about 1% of bank loans (S&P, reportedly) [S44]; reserve margin
  28–36% for 2024–2030 and low water stress (AMRO) [S7]; BNM's FSR 2H2025 mentions data centres only as
  construction [S10].

**What still supports the core (keep alongside):**

- No data-centre line in the EIA Order; the nearest trigger has no public display [S2].
- No project-level notice in planned areas [S3].
- The national guideline shows no objection step in the text extracted [S4].
- No data-centre-specific participation rule found; the Bill has no draft [S17].
- BMI's 90% projection, with its base-case caveat [S42].
- Ireland ran a 30-month public process and requires on-site or proximate generation for new data
  centres [S9, S50]. Malaysia "encourages" self-generation [S11].
- The Energy Commission revised projections to reduce stranded-asset and cost-shifting risk, per the
  op-ed, with its ramp-up caveat [S46].

### Misleading-framing risks

**(a) Malaysian government**
1. **Voice gap without the local-plan stage.** The brief says the s21(6) objection right "switches off where a local plan exists" but never says why. TCPA s12A, s13 (at least four weeks for objections, notice in three issues of two newspapers), s14 (local inquiry) and s16(3) (same steps for plan alterations) give the public a formal objection stage on zoning. s22(4)(a) bars permission that contravenes the plan. Leaving this out makes the voice gap look larger than the statute makes it. The fair claim is narrower: no *project-level* notice in planned areas, and no data-centre-specific EIA line.
2. **"Order" framing is chronologically false** (C1). The general voice rules predate the data-centre cost rules. What the record supports is an asymmetry of *specificity*, not of sequence.
3. **Fast tracks presuppose conforming land.** The 99- and 29-day tracks require consistency with land conditions and an approved layout or master plan. "No objection step" without this reads as if any data centre can be approved in 29 days anywhere.
4. **Hook misdescribes who decides** (C4). The local council grants planning permission. The DCTF assesses utilities. Malaysia also has no local ballot mechanism for any land use, so the contrast singles out data centres unfairly.
5. **Household protection beside data-centre costs.** Placing "household bill protection ends 31 Dec 2026" under the Economic lens "against" column implies the expiry is linked to data-centre costs. No source links them.
6. **23,500MW vs 12.3GW.** Many of the 27 earliest-stage projects expect off-grid supply (row 35). Setting the two numbers side by side overstates grid exposure.

**(b) Google**
1. **Title and slug imply displacement** ("voted out ... in"). No source links Google's Malaysia plan to US votes. The plan builds on a 2024 investment, and AP cites US$15.1tn of forecast US capex. The brief concedes this in WHAT WEAKENS, but the headline and hook carry the opposite impression.
2. **Johor grievances beside a non-Johor investor.** BMI's 90%, Iskandar Puteri complaints and RM5b of Johor reservoirs sit next to a Google hook, yet no Google site is in Johor.
3. **Omitted Google commitments.** Household bill-reduction programmes in host states from 2027, river-basin replenishment, and per-site water and PUE disclosure are all in the same release (row 16).

**(c) US data-centre developer**
1. **"US voters" from 41 local contests**, 21 of them in Ohio. Thirteen of the 40 were defeats of pro-development measures, seven of them non-binding, and the results are unofficial.
2. **Market juxtaposition.** Running the votes beside "Nasdaq -5.5%" suggests the ballots moved markets. AP attributes the risk of pauses to Democratic victories, plus borrowing costs and bubble fears.
3. **"Ban" vocabulary.** The 12 "partial bans" include size limits; three moratoriums last six months (row 83).

**(d) Johor resident**
1. **One changed voice stands in for the community.** Ramli Paiman's "not worried" quote appears under WHAT WEAKENS. The same article has him asking for job priority, and the Iskandar Puteri complaints are three days old.
2. **"Residents can and did stop a project"** (row 60) uses a Selangor case with a feasibility-review explanation. No Johor case shows a resident objection changing an outcome.
3. **Stale screening data.** "Johor screens and refuses about 30%" is Jun–Oct 2024 data, presented under a 2026 heading.
4. **Public money vs user-pays.** RM5b of public reservoir spending in Johor sits beside "developers fund water infrastructure". Neither the brief nor the sources say how much of that RM5b serves data centres. Either claim alone can mislead.
