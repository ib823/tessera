## 1. The amendment

1.1 **Name and vote.** It was the Melaka State Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2026, passed on 14 Jul 2026 by 23 votes to 5. [1] "The Melaka State Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2026 was passed with the support of 23 assemblymen, while five voted against it."

1.2 **Enacted name and number.** It is "Enakmen Perlembagaan Negeri Melaka (Pindaan) 2026 (Enakmen A44)". [11]

1.3 **Who voted against.** [2] "Five Pakatan Harapan (PH) representatives — four from DAP and one from Amanah — voted against the amendment." NST also says the 23 included "three from the opposition". See contradiction C1.

1.4 **Articles amended.** [3] "Fasal 2 bertujuan untuk meminda Perkara 11 Perlembagaan Negeri Melaka, untuk memasukkan bilangan Adun yang dilantik tidak lebih tujuh ahli." The bill also creates new Articles 11A, 11B and 11C. [4] "...changes to Article 11 of the Melaka State Constitution, and the creation of new Articles 11A, 11B and 11C..."

1.5 **Elected seats unchanged.** [2] "...the existing 28 elected assemblymen will remain unchanged..."

1.6 **Appointment procedure (Art. 11A).** [2] "...each appointed assemblyman must first be approved through a motion supported by at least seven assemblymen and passed by a simple majority before being appointed by the Yang di-Pertua Negeri." Appointees must live in Melaka. [2] "...only individuals residing in Melaka would be eligible..."

1.7 **Termination (Art. 11B).**
- [2] "...the Yang di-Pertua Negeri, acting on the advice of the Chief Minister, may terminate the appointment... without giving any reason..."
- [3] The government's determination "adalah muktamad serta tidak boleh dicabar di mahkamah" (is final and cannot be challenged in court).

1.8 **Term.** A seat is vacated on resignation, on a removal motion (7 movers plus a simple majority), on dissolution of the assembly, or on termination under 11B. [2] "...if the state assembly is dissolved, or if their appointment is terminated under Article 11B..." In practice the term cannot outlast the assembly.

1.9 **Voting rights are not fully settled.**
- [2] Appointees "have the right to speak, participate in debates and assembly committees, and vote, subject to the prescribed limits."
- [3] "Pindaan tersebut bagaimanapun tidak menyatakan secara jelas sama ada ahli yang dilantik itu turut boleh mengundi dalam usul undi percaya atau tidak percaya." (The amendment does not state clearly whether appointees can vote on confidence or no-confidence motions.)
- Art. 11C applies Articles 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 21 and 25 "mutatis mutandis". [3]
- Senior exco Rais Yasin said on 15 Jul that "elaun, hak mengundi dan sebagainya" (allowances, voting rights and so on) were still to be discussed. [7]

1.10 **Barred from top posts.** Appointees "are not eligible to be appointed as Chief Minister or State Executive Councillor (Exco)". [2]

1.11 **The cap is a ceiling, not a quota.** Ab Rauf, quoted by RTM [6]: "Jumlah itu merupakan had maksimum dan bukannya kewajipan untuk melantik kesemua tujuh ADUN berkenaan." (Seven is a maximum, not an obligation.)

1.12 **Chief Minister Ab Rauf Yusoh's rationale (quote).** [1] "The appointments can enhance the participation of women, youth, Orang Asli, minority communities, professionals and industry representatives who may not succeed through the electoral process..." He also called it "not to create a 'back door' to political power". [1]

1.13 **No appointees named.**
- [1] "Ab Rauf, however, said no appointed assemblymen would be named anytime soon. 'We can do that but we are almost at the end of the current term...'"
- The assembly was dissolved on 23 Sep (fact 3.1), which would vacate any appointed seat under fact 1.8.
- I found no report of any appointment.

1.14 **Gazette date.** It was reported as gazetted on 27 Aug, but only as the plaintiffs' statement. [12] "...the amendment, passed on July 14 and gazetted on Aug 27..." The same date appears in [10], [11] and [13]. I could not open the gazette itself.

1.15 **Origin in BN's 2021 manifesto, which promised five, not seven.** [21] (10 Nov 2021) "...appointing five additional state assemblymen from the youth and NGO background..." RTM [6] confirms the idea came from the BN 2021 manifesto, "Teras 1: Kestabilan Politik".

1.16 **Rais Yasin on PH's earlier stance.** [7] "...sebelum ini juga pernah ada cadangan mengenai Adun lantikan daripada pemimpin PH." (PH leaders had previously proposed appointed seats.) See fact 5.6.

## 2. The legal challenge

2.1 **Plaintiffs, defendant, court, filing date.** [10] (Bernama, 12 Oct) "The suit was filed by Kota Melaka MP Khoo Poay Tiong, in his capacity as a registered voter in Melaka, and Melaka PH chairman Adly Zahari... The Melaka state government is named as the defendant." Also: "filed at the Melaka High Court last Thursday (Oct 8)."

2.2 **Provisions relied on.** [10] "...contravened Article 71(4) of the Federal Constitution, read together with Section 4(1) of Part I of the Eighth Schedule... and was therefore null and void."

2.3 **Relief sought.** The plaintiffs seek declarations that the amendment:
- is void under Federal Constitution Art. 4(1);
- violates the basic structure of the Melaka State Constitution, making it void under "Perkara 27 Perlembagaan Negeri Melaka" [11];
- violates the basic structure of the Federal Constitution [10].

2.4 **Timing.** [13] "...we were keeping an eye out for the gazette because we can only start the court process after it's gazetted." Khoo said the filing near the election was "purely coincidental". [13]

2.5 **Hearing date or ruling.** None found.

## 3. The election

All of 3.1 to 3.4 come from Election Commission (SPR) press statements.

3.1 **Dissolution.** [P1] (22 Sep 2026) "Pembubaran Dewan Undangan Negeri (DUN) Melaka yang ke-15 pada 23 September 2026 sebagaimana termaktub di bawah Fasal (2) Perkara 19 Perlembagaan Negeri Melaka." Polls are required within 60 days.

3.2 **Key dates.** [P2] (7 Oct 2026) "Tarikh Writ: 7 Oktober 2026 (Rabu); Hari Penamaan Calon: 31 Oktober 2026 (Sabtu); Hari Undi Awal: 10 November 2026 (Selasa); Hari Mengundi: 14 November 2026 (Sabtu)." (Writ 7 Oct, nomination 31 Oct, early voting 10 Nov, polling 14 Nov.)

3.3 **Seats and voters.** [P2] "...mengisi kekosongan 28 kerusi..." (filling 28 seats) and "...687,697 orang pemilih yang terdiri daripada 675,166 orang pengundi biasa, 9,769 orang tentera serta pasangan, dan 2,762 orang polis." (687,697 voters: 675,166 ordinary voters, 9,769 military personnel and spouses, 2,762 police.)

3.4 **Candidates.** [P3] (31 Oct 2026) "...seramai 79 calon mewakili pelbagai parti disahkan bertanding..." (79 candidates confirmed.)
- By party: PH 24, BN 23, Bersama 16, Bersatu 7, PN 5, Putra 1, Independents 3.
- No nominations were rejected.
- Contests: 11 straight fights, 11 three-cornered, 6 four-cornered.

## 4. 2021 baseline

4.1 **Result.** [14] (Bernama, 31 Oct 2026) "Barisan Nasional (BN) secured 21 seats... UMNO winning 18, MCA two and MIC one... more than two-thirds majority. Pakatan Harapan (PH) won five seats, with DAP securing four and Amanah one, while Perikatan Nasional (PN) took two."

4.2 **Two-thirds rule.** Constitutional amendments need two-thirds of "the total number of members". [P4] Eighth Schedule s.19(4): "...supported on Second and third Readings by the votes of not less than two-thirds of the total number of members thereof."

4.3 **What two-thirds means in practice (my arithmetic, not a source).**
- Two-thirds of 28 is 18.67, so 19 seats.
- Two-thirds of 35 is 23.33, so 24 seats.
- BN's 2021 haul of 21 seats plus 7 appointees would be 28 of 35.
- Whether appointees count toward "total number of members" under the Melaka text is UNVERIFIED.

## 6. Federal Constitution text

Source [P4]: official reprint, version 2010, hosted by WIPO Lex: https://wipolex-res.wipo.int/edocs/lexdocs/laws/en/my/my063en.pdf

6.1 **Art. 71(4).** "if at any time the Constitution of any State does not contain the provisions set out in part i of the eighth Schedule... or contains provisions inconsistent with the essential provisions, parliament may... by law make provision for giving effect in that State to the essential provisions or for removing the inconsistent provisions."
- Note: the clause's remedy is federal legislation by Parliament.

6.2 **Eighth Schedule, Part I, s.4(1)** (reprint p.182): "the Legislative Assembly shall consist of such number of elected members as the Legislature may by law provide." Section 4(2) is marked "(Repealed)".

6.3 **Part II, s.21(1)(b)** (p.191) allowed "such number of other members, being less than the number of elected members, as the Ruler may appoint". Art. 71(5)(a) permits that substitution only "until the dissolution of the second Legislative Assembly". Section 22 substitutes the Yang di-Pertua Negeri for the Ruler in Malacca and Penang.

6.4 **Sabah and Sarawak.** Art. 71(7)(a): "Clause (5) shall not apply." Those two states have a separate basis.

6.5 **Expert reading.** Shad Saleem Faruqi [17] argues the s.21 window expired after Melaka's second assembly (1964–69): "It is now 57 years too late to employ Section 21(1)(b) of the Eighth Schedule to legislate for nominated assembly members." He also sets out the proponents' reliance on s.21 and the Sabah precedent.

## 8. Contradictions

- **C1. Opposition votes for the bill.** NST [2] says 23 "including three from the opposition". Bernama [14] gives PN only 2 seats in 2021, and Malaysiakini [3] names only Yadzil. 23 = BN 21 + PN 2 is consistent with the 2021 seat counts, so NST's "three" looks doubtful.
- **C2. Voting method.** The Vibes [4] says "show of hands"; Malaysiakini [3] says "undian suara" (voice vote).
- **C3. Type of suit.** The Star [12] says "originating summons"; Bernama [10], FMT [13] and The Edge say "writ of summons".
- **C4. Eighth Schedule citation.** The Star [12] says "Section 4 of Part I"; Bernama [10] and Malaysiakini [11] say "Section 4(1)".
- **C5. Adly's capacity as plaintiff.** The Star [12] says "assemblyman at the time of filing". FMT [13] says "at the time the constitutional amendment bill was passed". The assembly was dissolved on 23 Sep [P1], before the 8 Oct filing, so the FMT version fits.
- **C6. Johor amendment date.** The Star [34] dates it 7 May 2026; FMT [13] says "in June". The Star is contemporaneous.
- **C7. Whether appointees can vote on confidence motions.**
  - NST [2]: "subject to the prescribed limits".
  - Malaysiakini [3]: the text is silent on confidence votes.
  - Rais Yasin [7]: voting rights are "to be discussed".
  - Khoo [5] and Faruqi [17] assume appointees can vote.
  - Unresolved without the gazette.
- **C8. MCA and PN seat counts.**
  - The Star, 30 Oct: MCA 3 seats and PN 6.
  - Bernama, 31 Oct [14b] and The Star, 6 Nov [24]: MCA 4.
  - SPR [P3] lists PN 5 plus Bersatu 7 separately; Malaysiakini [25] also says PN 5.
- **C9. Pledged versus enacted cap.** The 2021 BN manifesto pledged five [21]; the 2026 law allows seven [1].
