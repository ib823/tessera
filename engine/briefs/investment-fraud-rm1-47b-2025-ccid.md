# BRIEF — Investment fraud losses RM1.47B in 2025 (CCID)

**Slug:** investment-fraud-rm1-47b-2025-ccid
**Proposed issue ID:** 2004
**Radar provenance:** queue #104 (silence_anomaly), first_seen 2026-04-19; develop-list pick A5
**Source date:** 2026-05-31

## ISSUE
Royal Malaysia Police (Bukit Aman Commercial Crime Investigation Department, CCID)
reported that non-existent investment scams cost Malaysians RM1.47 billion across
9,603 cases in 2025 — up from RM848.6 million across 6,337 cases in 2024. That is a
~73% jump in losses and ~52% jump in cases in a single year, even as public
anti-scam awareness spending rose. The editorial gap: detection and enforcement sit
*downstream* of the loss (police take the report after the money is gone), while the
syndicate's actual distribution channel — social media platforms — carries no
domestic liability for hosting the recruitment funnel. The story is not "scams are
bad"; it is that the loss curve is accelerating while the control points remain
post-loss.

## PERIOD
- **2024 baseline:** 6,337 non-existent-investment cases, RM848.6M losses (CCID).
- **2025:** 9,603 cases, RM1.47B losses (CCID).
- **18 Apr 2026:** Bukit Aman CCID director Datuk Rusdi Mohd Isa briefs the 2025
  figures and the five-method taxonomy. (Reported widely 18–19 Apr 2026.)
- Context: total scam losses (all categories) ran ~RM2.7B as of Nov 2025 (police);
  Bukit Aman separately said ~90% of commercial crime is now online (24 Feb 2026).

## ACTORS
- **Datuk Rusdi Mohd Isa** — Director, Bukit Aman Commercial Crime Investigation
  Department (CCID). Source of the 2025 figures and method breakdown.
- **Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) / CCID** — receives reports, investigates, runs the
  Semak Mule account-checking and the National Scam Response Centre (NSRC, 997).
- **Securities Commission Malaysia (SC)** — capital-market licensing regulator;
  maintains the Investor Alert List of unlicensed/clone entities.
- **Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM)** — financial-stability and payment-system oversight.
- **Social media platforms (Meta/Facebook, Instagram; Telegram; WhatsApp)** — the
  recruitment-and-grooming channel named by CCID. No Malaysian platform-liability
  statute attaches loss to the host.
- **Victims** — working-age adults; CCID says the 31–50 band (especially 41–50) is
  hardest hit, then 51–60, then 21–30.

## RELEVANT LAW
- **Penal Code s.420** (cheating) — primary charging provision for scam operators.
- **Anti-Money Laundering, Anti-Terrorism Financing and Proceeds of Unlawful
  Activities Act 2001 (AMLA)** — mule-account and proceeds tracing.
- **Capital Markets and Services Act 2007 (CMSA)** — SC's power over unlicensed
  capital-market activity (s.7 licensing; investor-alert / cease-and-desist).
- **Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 (CMA), s.233** — content takedown route
  (MCMC), but no host-liability-for-loss regime.
- No Malaysian equivalent (as at the brief date) of a statutory platform duty to
  reimburse authorised-push-payment / investment-scam losses (contrast: UK PSR
  mandatory APP-fraud reimbursement, live Oct 2024).

## THE FIVE METHODS (CCID taxonomy, 18 Apr 2026)
1. Fake companies / **clone firms** (impersonating licensed entities).
2. Promises of **quick, high returns**.
3. **Ponzi** structures (early "profits" paid from later victims' deposits).
4. **Love scams** routed into investment.
5. **Bogus crypto** platforms.
**Funnel:** lure on Facebook/Instagram → move to WhatsApp/Telegram → add to a fake
"investor group" with a supposed professional trader → victim sees fabricated
profits on a fake dashboard → cannot withdraw → is told to pay "processing fees,
taxes or other charges" → second loss.

## CONTRADICTIONS
- **"RM1.3bil" vs "RM1.47bil" (2025):** an early-Jan-2026 CCID statement put 2025
  fake-investment losses at "over RM1.3bil"; the 18 Apr 2026 full-year briefing gave
  RM1.47B/9,603. The April figure is the later, full-year, more authoritative one —
  use RM1.47B and note it supersedes the interim RM1.3B. [S1 vs S9]
- **"RM2.7B" / "RM2.9B" all-scam totals** are *all-category* scam losses, a larger
  superset — do NOT conflate with the RM1.47B investment-only figure. Keep the
  denominator explicit.

## EDITORIAL ANGLE (for Stage 1)
Lead with a named, identifiable mechanic (the "can't-withdraw + pay-a-fee" second
hit) and the YoY acceleration, then reframe from "scams are rising" to "every control
point is downstream of the loss, and the recruitment channel bears no liability."
View: awareness campaigns target the victim's judgement; the money moves through
platforms and mule accounts that the law still treats as bystanders.
