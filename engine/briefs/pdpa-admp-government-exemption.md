# RESEARCH BRIEF — Algorithmic-accountability guidelines vs the PDPA government exemption

**Slug:** `pdpa-admp-government-exemption`
**Issue ID:** 2008
**Author:** Stage 0/1 (Claude) · **Date:** 2026-06-05
**Source channel:** Digital Policy Alert (`digitalpolicyalert.org`) silent-issue pass

---

## ISSUE

On 30 April 2026 the Department of Personal Data Protection (JPDP), under the
Ministry of Digital, adopted Malaysia's first three operative data-governance
guidelines — Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA), Data Protection by
Design, and **Automated Decision-Making & Profiling (ADMP)**. The DPIA
guideline makes *any* automated decision-making or profiling an automatic
trigger for a mandatory impact assessment, regardless of how many people it
affects. Yet the entire framework rests on the Personal Data Protection Act
2010 (Act 709), whose Section 3 excludes the Federal and State Governments
from the Act altogether. The result: the new algorithmic-accountability
regime binds every private firm that profiles a customer, while the largest
automated profiler of Malaysians — the state's own central database, PADU,
holding records on 30.7 million people — sits outside the law it is issued
under.

## PERIOD / TIMELINE

- **15 Jun 2010** — PDPA 2010 (Act 709) gazetted; Section 3 excludes Federal
  and State Governments from the Act. [S1]
- **2 Jan 2024** — PADU (Pangkalan Data Utama / Central Database Hub) launched
  by then-Economy Minister Rafizi Ramli; all citizens/PRs 18+ asked to verify
  39 fields; system integrates 600+ data points from across government on 30.7
  million individuals. [S9][S10]
- **17 Jul 2024** — Digital Minister Gobind Singh Deo confirms PADU is "not
  bound by Act 709" because the Act "regulates commercial transactions." [S8]
- **Oct 2024** — PDPA (Amendment) Act 2024 (Act A1727) passed; Section 3
  government exemption retained (not among the 5 key reforms enacted from 22
  consulted). [S2][S12]
- **1 Jan 2025** — Amendment Act enters into force (staggered). [S2]
- **25 Feb 2025** — JPDP issues DPO-appointment and data-breach-notification
  guidelines. [S2]
- **Jun 2025** — Phase 3 obligations live: mandatory DPO, 72-hour breach
  notification (new s.12B), data portability. [S2]
- **Mar–Apr 2025** — JPDP public consultations on DPIA (PCP 1/2025), Data
  Protection by Design, and ADMP (PCP 3/2025). [S3][S4]
- **30 Apr 2026** — Final DPIA, Data Protection by Design, and ADMP guidelines
  adopted (v1.0). DPIA mandatory where >20,000 data subjects, or >10,000 with
  sensitive data, OR any ADMP regardless of volume; DPIA valid 2 years. [S3][S4][S5]

## ACTORS

- **JPDP / Personal Data Protection Commissioner** — issuer of the guidelines;
  enforcement authority over the private sector under Act 709.
- **Ministry of Digital** — parent ministry; Minister Gobind Singh Deo.
- **PADU / Ministry of Economy** — operator of the central database; launched
  under Rafizi Ramli.
- **Cabinet** — rejected a proposal to remove the Section 3 exemption. [S12]
- **Malaysian Bar** — issued a press statement on the public-trust deficit
  from government data handling and the absence of PDPA recourse. [S11]
- **Data subjects** — 30.7 million people in PADU; 22.5 million whose details
  were exposed in the 2022 leak with no PDPA remedy against any agency. [S7][S9]

## RELEVANT LAW

- **PDPA 2010 (Act 709), Section 3** — "This Act shall not apply to the Federal
  Government and State Governments." [S1] Verbatim exclusion.
- **PDPA (Amendment) Act 2024 (Act A1727)** — added mandatory DPO, 72-hour
  breach notification (s.12B), data portability; did NOT amend Section 3. [S2]
- **JPDP Guidelines (30 Apr 2026)** — DPIA, Data Protection by Design, ADMP.
  These are *guidelines* (operational guidance), not standalone statute; the
  DPIA obligation derives from the amended Act, the guidelines specify when and
  how. [S3][S4][S5] PDP Commissioner: Shariffah Rashidah Syed Othman. ADMP adds
  a data-subject "Right to Refuse" solely-automated decisions with significant
  effect, and bars profiling of children for marketing. [S16]
- **Malaysia's public-sector data laws (the state is NOT lawless on data):**
  - **Cyber Security Act 2024 (Act 854)** — in force 26 Aug 2024; NACSA; 11
    NCII sectors including "Government"; mandatory risk assessments, audits,
    incident notification. Protects state systems from cyber threats. [S17]
  - **Data Sharing Act 2025 (Act 864)** — Royal Assent 5 Feb 2025, in force 28
    Apr 2025; binds Federal Government agencies and federal statutory bodies;
    National Data Sharing Committee; **Section 18: up to RM1 million / 5 years**
    for officers who misuse or disclose shared data without authority. [S18]
  These laws guard data security and punish leaking officers; **neither grants a
  data subject any right to an algorithmic impact assessment, an explanation, or
  to refuse an automated state decision.** That individual-recourse gap is the
  issue, not a claim that the government is unregulated.
- **Comparators:**
  - **Singapore** — PDPA excludes the public sector, BUT the Public Sector
    (Governance) Act 2018 (PSGA) governs it with criminal penalties for
    officers (unauthorised disclosure, misuse for gain, re-identification);
    PSGA amended Jan 2026. [S6]
  - **EU GDPR Article 22** — right not to be subject to a solely automated
    decision with legal/significant effect, with human-intervention safeguards;
    binds public authorities. [S13]

## KEY STATISTICS (each with primary-leaning citation)

- **30.7 million** socio-economic profiles in PADU as of April 2026 (citizens +
  PRs), integrating data from **200+** agencies; **600+** integrated data points;
  **39** fields individuals self-verify. [S9; S15 SAYS citing Economy Ministry; S10]
- **PADU "Analytics-as-a-Service"** rolled out from **May 2026** to **26**
  government agencies; use cases stated by Economy Minister Akmal Nasrullah
  include scoring a borrower's **capability to repay a government loan** and
  ageing-population targeting. PADU is, by function, an automated-profiling
  engine. [S19 The Star / S20 Malay Mail / FMT, 20-21 Apr 2026]
- **52.6%** (11.55 million) of citizens 18+ actively registered/updated PADU by
  the 31 Mar 2024 deadline; Rafizi later cited **17.65 million** profiles
  updated by 1 Apr 2024. CONTRADICTION noted below. [S9]
- **22.5 million** Malaysians (aged 18–82, born 1940–2004) whose MyKad, name,
  DOB, address and phone were offered for sale in May 2022, allegedly via JPN's
  MyIdentity API; Home Minister denied NRD was the source. No PDPA recourse
  against any government agency. [S7][S11]
- **DPIA thresholds:** >20,000 data subjects, OR >10,000 with sensitive data,
  OR **any** ADMP regardless of volume. DPIA valid 2 years. [S3]
- **Section 3 government exemption:** Malaysia and Singapore are reported as the
  only countries with such a blanket statutory carve-out; Cabinet rejected a
  proposal to remove it. [S12]
- **72 hours** — breach-notification deadline imposed on the private sector
  (s.12B, Jun 2025) — an obligation the government is not subject to. [S2]

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **PADU active-registration figure.** 11.55 million / 52.6% of adults
  "registered and updated" by the 31 Mar 2024 deadline (Economy Ministry press
  line) vs Rafizi's 17.65 million "profiles updated" by 1 Apr 2024. These
  measure different things (citizen-verified vs profiles touched). AUTHORITATIVE
  CHOICE: do **not** use the active-registration number as the headline scale —
  use the system-coverage figure (30.7 million individuals integrated), because
  PADU is pre-populated from existing government databases regardless of whether
  a citizen logs in. The coverage figure is the load-bearing one for the issue.
- **2022 breach source.** Hackers claimed the JPN MyIdentity API; the Home
  Minister denied NRD was the source and said the data was aggregated from other
  sellers. AUTHORITATIVE FRAMING: report it as an *alleged* leak of 22.5 million
  records that the government denied originated from NRD — the load-bearing point
  is the *absence of PDPA recourse against any agency*, which holds regardless
  of source. Do not assert NRD was the source.

## CORRECTION (post-draft, external deep-research report, primary-verified)

- **"Malaysia passed no equivalent" was WRONG.** The Stage 1 draft's Governance
  card said Singapore pairs its public-sector exemption with an accountability
  law while "Malaysia passed no equivalent." Malaysia DOES have public-sector
  data statutes: the **Cyber Security Act 2024** and the **Data Sharing Act
  2025** (the latter binds the Federal Government with RM1m / 5-year officer
  penalties under s.18). Corrected the card to the accurate gap: those laws
  guard data security and punish leaking officers, but **none gives a citizen an
  algorithmic-accountability right** (DPIA on state systems, explanation, or a
  right to refuse an automated state decision). Verified against Act 864 [S18]
  and Act 854 [S17] primary texts.
- **Singapore comparator weakened and dropped from the card.** Singapore also
  exempts its public sector and handles public-sector algorithms via internal AI
  governance, not statutory DPIAs, so it is not a clean "did it right" foil.
  Kept in the brief for context; removed from the reader card to avoid a
  misleading contrast.

## THE T4A ANGLE (for Stage 1)

Malaysia just issued its first rulebook holding algorithms accountable for
decisions about people, and pointed it only at the private sector. The state's
own automated decisions (subsidy eligibility, welfare targeting, and from May
2026 the loan-repayment scoring PADU runs for 26 agencies over 30.7 million
profiles) sit outside the Act the rulebook is built on, because Section 3
excludes the Federal and State Governments. The exemption is no oversight: JPDP's
2020 review weighed extending the Act to government, and the 2024 reform still
left Section 3 untouched. Malaysia is NOT lawless on state data, the Cyber
Security Act 2024 and Data Sharing Act 2025 guard it (the latter jails officers
who leak it), but none gives a citizen an algorithmic-accountability right: no
DPIA on state systems, no explanation, no right to refuse. After 22.5 million
records were put up for sale in 2022, no one had a data-protection remedy against
any agency, whoever the source. Emotion: anger-at-process / anxiety-of-precedent,
not the leak, the rulebook that exempts the biggest profiler from the rules it
imposes on everyone else.
