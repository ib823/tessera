## ISSUE

**Editor's thesis after research (changed).** The working thesis had four parts. Two held, one was wrong, and one
needs precise wording.

1. **HELD: a KL company was designated.** On 5 November 2026 OFAC designated four individuals and 41 entities in
   Prince Group's network under E.O. 13581, plus one person under E.O. 13694 [S1]. One of the 41 is
   **Greenmeadows Properties Sdn Bhd**, "Malaysia-based", for which Dai An, "a high-level leader in the Prince Group
   TCO" designated on 23 June 2026, "serves as director" [S1]. The SDN entry gives a Kuala Lumpur address (Taman Tun
   Dr Ismail), "Organization Established Date 03 Dec 2018", type "Activities of holding companies", registration
   number 201801044010, "(Linked To: DAI, An)" [S2]; the Federal Register notice (91 FR 72627) designates it under
   s. 1(a)(ii)(C) of E.O. 13581 [S3]. Of the 41 entities, 23 are Cambodian, 8 BVI, 6 Hong Kong, 2 Singapore, 1
   Mauritius and **1 Malaysian** (FORA count of the SDN entries) [S2]. Neither earlier Prince Group round (146
   targets, 14 Oct 2025; 35 targets, 23 Jun 2026) carries a Malaysian address or nationality on OFAC's listing pages
   [S5a, S5b, S4a, S4b].
2. **HELD: a Malaysian national was designated the same day, for Madagascar.** OFAC designated Chui Kok Wa under
   E.O. 13694 (cyber), saying he "led an extensive network of scammers located in Madagascar" that defrauded
   Americans "of over $400,000 equivalent" [S1]. The SDN entry lists "nationality Malaysia", place of birth Negeri
   Sembilan, address Antananarivo [S2, S3]. **He is not part of the Prince Group designation**: different
   authority ([CYBER4], not [TCO]), different network [S2].
3. **WRONG: "Malaysian coverage ran wire stories about a 'Cambodian scam group' and did not name" the Malaysian
   end.** Every Malaysian report of the 5 Nov action that we opened (six: The Star twice, The Sun, The Vibes, RTM,
   Oriental Daily) named Chui Kok Wa as Malaysian; The Vibes put him in its subheadline and Oriental Daily in its
   headline [S16a-f]. Kosmo had already called him a Malaysian "bos 'scammer'" on 31 Oct [S17c]. **What held:
   none of the six named Greenmeadows or any Malaysian company**. Three of the six headlines named Cambodia (The
   Star 6 Nov, The Sun, The Vibes); only Oriental Daily's headline named a Malaysian, the man [S16]. So the gap is
   narrower than first framed: Malaysian readers were told about the Malaysian national accused abroad, not about
   the Malaysian company at home.
4. **NEEDS PRECISE WORDING: "Malaysia gives legal effect to UN sanctions, not unilateral US ones."** True as
   policy and as freezing law, but Malaysian law is not silent:
   - Ministers have said Malaysia recognises only UN sanctions: PM Anwar, 7 Nov 2023 (The Star's reported
     speech: Malaysia "only recognises sanctions by the United Nations Security Council and not unilateral
     sanctions by the United States") [S15b]; Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution, 9 May 2024, to US Treasury's
     Brian Nelson ("we will only recognise sanctions if they are imposed by the United Nations Security Council")
     [S15a]; MITI, 4 Mar 2025 ("Malaysia maintains a neutral position on unilateral sanctions") [S12].
   - Freezing duties for banks attach only to the Domestic List and UNSCR List (BNM policy document para 27.6)
     [S9 p. 115]. The Domestic List is the Home Minister's AMLA s. 66B list, which covers **terrorist acts only**
     [S8 pp. 99-100]; s. 66C implements UN Security Council measures [S8 p. 102].
   - **But** BNM's binding standard 27.8.3 says: "Reporting institutions shall submit a suspicious transaction
     report if there is any positive name match with individuals or entities listed in other unilateral sanctions
     lists" ("S" = must be complied with; non-compliance "may result in enforcement action") [S9 pp. 15, 117].
     Para 27.3.11 (guidance) says banks "may monitor and consolidate other countries' unilateral sanctions lists"
     [S9 p. 114].
   - AMLA s. 44 lets an enforcement agency freeze property once "an investigation with regard to an unlawful
     activity has commenced" and it has reasonable grounds to suspect proceeds; "unlawful activity" includes a
     "foreign serious offence" certified by the foreign State [S8 pp. 10-11, 14, 66-67]. A freeze therefore needs a
     Malaysian investigation, not Malaysian recognition of the US list. **Do not write "Malaysia cannot freeze".**
   - MACMA 2002 lets the Attorney General assist a prescribed foreign State to enforce a foreign **court**
     forfeiture order, or to restrain property where such an order "may be made in judicial proceedings" [S10 pp.
     8, 32]. An OFAC designation is administrative, not a court order.

**Malaysian response: none found.** No statement from PDRM, the Home Ministry, BNM, SSM, Wisma Putra or the AGC
on Greenmeadows, Chui Kok Wa, or any of the three Prince Group rounds was found as at 18 Nov 2026 (search log in
VERIFICATION GAPS). This is a finding about public statements only: suspicious transaction reports and
investigations are confidential (AMLA s. 14, s. 35 tipping-off) [S8 pp. 32, 61-62].

**The comparator that sharpens the point.** Singapore also "does not enforce unilateral sanctions imposed by other
countries" [S14a, S14b]. Yet Singapore Police, which had STRO intelligence on Chen Zhi from 2024, said that "with
the additional information obtained from the US's and UK's press releases on 14 Oct 2025" they advanced the
investigation, seized or prohibited more than S$150 million in assets on 30 Oct 2025 [S13a], and by 3 Mar 2026 had
arrested three Singaporeans, with assets "seized and/or prohibited" worth "more than S$500 million" [S13b]. MITI's
own 2025 statement shows Malaysia uses the same move elsewhere: on US-sanctioned chips, "the Government of Malaysia
is taking the necessary actions to establish whether Malaysian laws had been breached" [S12].

**Developable core (anger at process, not at a community or at named officials).** A KL-registered company is now
on a US list as part of a scam network's corporate structure. Malaysia's UN-only stance answers whether Malaysia
must freeze it (no). It does not answer whether Malaysian banks filed the report BNM's own rules require on a
match, or whether any Malaysian agency opened a file, and nobody has said. The Malaysian end of the scam economy
includes a company registry entry and a Malaysian national abroad, not only victims. Differentiate from 2004
(prevention: mule accounts, platforms) and 2018 (victim compensation): this issue is about the supply side and the
corporate plumbing.

**Allegation discipline.** OFAC designations are administrative determinations; the Chui case is a criminal
complaint (not an indictment) [S7], an allegation he has not answered in any source found; FORA treats him as
presumed innocent. Greenmeadows is designated for being "owned or
controlled by, or [having] acted ... for or on behalf of" Dai An [S3]; **the release alleges no specific conduct by
Greenmeadows itself**, and the general sentences about concealing assets and evading sanctions describe the whole
action, not this company [S1]. Prince Group "has previously denied any involvement in cyberscams" (AFP) [S16a].
No statement from Greenmeadows or Chui was found.

## RELEVANT LAW

**United States (what a designation does).** All property and interests in property "subject to U.S.
jurisdiction" of designated persons are blocked and US persons are generally barred from dealing with them;
entities 50% or more owned by blocked persons are also blocked; "financial institutions and other persons may risk
exposure to sanctions for engaging in certain transactions" with them [S1, S3]. Greenmeadows: E.O. 13581 s.
1(a)(ii)(C) [S3 p. 72627]. Chui: E.O. 13694 s. 1(a)(ii)(D) [S3 p. 72626]. OFAC offers a delisting petition process
[S1].

**Malaysia.**
- **AMLA 2001 (Act 613)**, AGC updated text "As at 1 March 2026" [S8]:
  - s. 3 "unlawful activity": activity constituting "any serious offence or any foreign serious offence ...
    regardless whether such activity, wholly or partly, takes place within or outside Malaysia" (pp. 10 to 11).
    "foreign serious offence": an offence against a foreign State's law "stated in a certificate purporting to be
    issued by or on behalf of the government of that foreign State" that would be a serious offence in Malaysia
    (p. 14). "serious offence" includes any federal offence punishable by one year or more (p. 14).
  - s. 14(1)(b): a reporting institution "shall promptly report" a transaction where circumstances give reason to
    suspect proceeds of an unlawful activity (p. 32). s. 35: tipping-off offence (pp. 61 to 62).
  - **s. 44(1)** (Part VI, pp. 66 to 67): an enforcement agency "may issue an order to freeze any property of any
    person" if "(a) an investigation with regard to an unlawful activity has commenced against that person" and
    (b) it has reasonable grounds to suspect an AMLA offence, or that the property is "the proceeds of an unlawful
    activity or the instrumentalities of an offence".
  - **Part VIA** (not "Part VI" as the lead said), "Suppression of terrorism financing offences": **s. 66B** (pp. 99
    to 102): the Home Minister, "satisfied on information given to him by a police officer" that an entity
    committed or facilitated "a terrorist act", may declare it a specified entity by Gazette order; dealing in its
    property is an offence (fine up to RM3m or five years). **This domestic listing power is terrorism-only.**
    **s. 66C** (pp. 102 to 103): Gazette orders to give effect to UN Security Council Article 41 measures.
- **BNM, AML/CFT/CPF and TFS for FIs** (BNM/RH/PD 030-14, issued 5 Feb 2024, effective 6 Feb 2024) [S9]:
  - Legend: "S" denotes a requirement "that must be complied with. Non-compliance may result in enforcement
    action"; "G" denotes guidance (p. 15; also para 9.1, p. 17).
  - 27.1.1 (p. 112): "Domestic List" = entities declared under s. 66B(1). 27.4.1 (p. 114): screen customers
    "against the Domestic List and UNSCR List". 27.6.1 to 27.6.3 (p. 115): freeze or block on a confirmed match;
    freezing lasts "until the specified entity is removed from the Domestic List or UNSCR List".
  - **27.3.11 (G, p. 114)**: "Reporting institutions may monitor and consolidate other countries' unilateral
    sanctions lists in their sanctions database."
  - **27.8.3 (S, p. 117)**: "Reporting institutions shall submit a suspicious transaction report if there is any
    positive name match with individuals or entities listed in other unilateral sanctions lists." Same rule in
    28.7.3 (p. 122, proliferation) and 29.7.3 (p. 127, other UN regimes). Para 29 covers other UN regimes via
    Central Bank of Malaysia Act 2009 regulations (29.1.1, 29.2.1, p. 123); para 28 refers to the Strategic Trade
    Act 2010 (STA not opened separately).
  - **Precision:** the freeze duty does not reach an OFAC-only listing; the STR duty does. Whether 27.8.3 applies
    only to institutions that load unilateral lists (27.3.11 is optional) is not stated; ask BNM (GAP 2).
- **Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act 2002 (Act 621)**, reprint 2006 [S10]: "foreign forfeiture order" =
  "an order made by a court in a prescribed foreign State" (s. 2, p. 8); prescribed foreign State requires a treaty
  or agreement (s. 17, p. 17); s. 31 (p. 32): the AG may assist enforcement of a foreign forfeiture order, or
  restrain property where such an order "may be made in judicial proceedings". Later amendments not checked (GAP 6).
- **Penal Code (Act 574)** s. 4 (updated text as at 1 Feb 2018, pp. 32 to 33) [S11]: Chapters VI, VIA and VIB (VIB
  = "Organized Crime", s. 130U onward) apply to offences "by any citizen ... in any place without and beyond the
  limits of Malaysia". Cheating (Chapter XVII) is not listed in s. 4. Malaysia's reach over a citizen's fraud on
  foreigners abroad is therefore limited to the organised-crime chapter. **Off cards unless re-verified against
  the current AGC text (GAP 6).**
- **Policy (not statute)**: UN-only recognition [S15a, S15b, S12]; FM 2022 named the UN General Assembly [S15c]
  (CONTRADICTION 8).

**Singapore (comparator).** "Singapore does not enforce unilateral sanctions imposed by other countries" [S14a,
S14b]; money laundering under CDSA s. 54, up to 10 years or S$500,000 [S13a].

## WHAT WEAKENS THE THESIS

1. **Malaysian media did report the Malaysian end.** All six Malaysian reports opened named Chui [S16]; Kosmo
   called him a Malaysian "bos 'scammer'" before the sanctions [S17c]. Only the company went unnamed, and the
   Treasury gave it one sentence [S1].
2. **The company may be a shell with nothing here.** Treasury alleges no conduct by Greenmeadows; it is one of 41
   entities and the only Malaysian address among the 226 Prince Group targets across three rounds [S1, S2, S4a,
   S4b, S5a, S5b]. A holding company with
   a director in common is not proof of assets or activity in Malaysia.
3. **Silence is not inaction.** STRs and investigations are confidential (AMLA ss. 14, 35) [S8]; BNM 27.8.3 already
   obliges banks to report a match [S9]. Malaysia may have acted without saying so.
4. **The UN-only stance is old, cross-government and shared.** Stated in 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 [S15, S12];
   Singapore holds the same line [S14]. It is not a Prince Group-specific choice.
5. **Singapore is an imperfect comparator.** Its case began in 2024 from STRO intelligence and targeted assets and
   people in Singapore [S13a, S13b]; no source shows Prince Group assets in Malaysia.
6. **Malaysia's reach over Chui is narrow.** His alleged victims are American; Penal Code s. 4 extends to citizens
   abroad only for Chapters VI, VIA, VIB [S11]; the US is the prosecuting state and has asked Madagascar, not
   Malaysia, to extradite [S6].
7. **The US case is untested.** A criminal complaint, not an indictment; designations are administrative; the FBI
   could not substantiate the workers' abuse claims and noted conduct "potentially inconsistent with claims of
   duress" [S7].
8. **Sovereignty and due process.** Acting on a foreign executive's list without a Malaysian hearing raises its own
   fairness questions; OFAC has a delisting route [S1].
9. **Malaysia acts on scams elsewhere** (mule accounts, NSRC; issues 2004 and 2018).
