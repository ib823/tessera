# BRIEF: Malaysia targets 80% rice self-sufficiency by 2030, but the rate fell to 52.9% in 2024

## ISSUE

The National Agrofood Policy 2021-2030 (NAP 2.0) projects rice self-sufficiency rising to 75% in 2025 and 80% by 2030. DOSM's Supply and Utilization Accounts show the actual ratio falling from 62.1% in 2020 to 56.2% in 2023 and 52.9% in 2024, and the competition regulator traces the decline back to about 70% in 2017. The issue argues that the target and the harvest are moving apart, leaving Malaysia dependent on rice exporters' policy decisions and a rising food import bill.

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **Date of India's export ban.** Card: July 2023. MyCC public summary (section 7.4): "India's export ban on non-basmati rice in July 2022" [3]. India's DGFT notification and press release could not be opened from this environment. The editor should confirm the date against India's own notification before relying on either; the MyCC sentence may itself be wrong.
- **Food versus agrofood imports.** DOSM's press statement gives 2024 food imports of RM93.8 billion [2]; the full SUA publication gives agrofood imports of RM93.7 billion across 12 categories [1]. Same release, slightly different aggregates.
- **SSR series vintage.** MyCC's table (from MAFS) gives 68.0% for 2018 and 62.4% for 2019 [3]; the cards cite MyCC's "about 70% in 2017". DOSM's 2020-2024 series matches MyCC for 2020-2023 [1]. No conflict for the years the cards use.
