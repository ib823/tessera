# BRIEF: POCA's two-year renewable detention orders, and the ouster clause the Federal Court struck down in 2022

**Issue ID:** 1292
**Slug:** poca-allows-2-year-renewable-detention-with-no-jud
**Source date:** 2026-04-08
**Backfill date:** 2026-11-08
**Brief status:** Source-list backfill. Issue cards unchanged. **The cards' central legal claim about s. 15B conflicts with 2022 Federal Court rulings; see CONTRADICTIONS.**

## ISSUE

The Prevention of Crime Act 1959 (POCA), as amended from 2014, lets the Prevention of Crime Board order a registered person detained for up to two years. The Board may renew the order "for a further period not exceeding two years at a time", with no stated cap on renewals [1, 2]. Section 15B, in the statute text, bars judicial review of the Board's discretionary decisions except on procedural compliance [1]. A five-member Federal Court majority upheld s. 15B in February 2021 [4]. That order was set aside on review in July 2021 [5]. In April 2022 the Federal Court held s. 15B, and later s. 4, unconstitutional [6, 7]. The cards, sourced 8 April 2026, describe s. 15B as an operative bar.

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **Federal Court on s. 15B, 2021 against 2022.** In *Rovin Joty Kodeeswaran v Lembaga Pencegahan Jenayah & Ors* (press summary of the majority, 19 Feb 2021), the majority held s. 15B(1) "can still survive the constitutional challenge" [4]. On 30 Jul 2021, in *Nivesh Nair Mohan v Dato' Abdul Razak Musa & Ors*, a review panel set aside the 19 Feb 2021 order on natural-justice grounds and ordered a rehearing [5]. On 14 Apr 2022, in *Dhinesh*, a Federal Court bench led by Nallini Pathmanathan FCJ declared s. 15B void [6]. On 25 Apr 2022 the rehearing bench, chaired by the Chief Justice, "unanimously" found ss. 4 and 15B unconstitutional [7]. **Judged authoritative:** the April 2022 decisions are later and come from a bench of equal size. The cards follow the superseded 2021 position, or the bare statute text.
- **Statute text against law as declared.** The AGC reprint [1] still prints s. 15B; reprints do not delete provisions a court declares void. The cards rely on the text alone.
- **History of the ouster clause.** Act A1459 [2] inserted the ouster clause and a s. 19A(2) providing that Board directions "shall be subject to review by the High Court". The reprint shows s. 19A(2) "(Deleted by Act A1549)", in force 15 Dec 2017 [1]. The cards do not use this history; it is noted for the editor because earlier drafts claimed the 2014 amendment removed judicial review.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- **6 Nov 2026:** The Star reports a Court of Appeal decision upholding a POCA curfew-breach sentence. It refers to "the Federal Court's 2022 ruling in the Nivesh Nair Mohan case" declaring s. 4 unconstitutional [8]. This confirms the 2022 ruling is still treated as good law; it does not change the cards' error, which predates publication.
