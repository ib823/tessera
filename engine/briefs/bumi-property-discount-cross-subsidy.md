## ISSUE

State governments set Bumiputera quotas and discounts on new private housing, and developers carry the cost of both.
The construction regulator's 2021 compliance-cost study, written with the developers' research arm, describes the
discount as cross-subsidised by open-market buyers. It also describes the discount as applied across the board, not
targeted, and documents 6,121 unsold and unreleased quota units in a June 2020 survey. The cards argue that the policy
question is the instrument (developer-administered cross-subsidy versus revenue-funded support), not the goal of
Bumiputera home ownership.

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **Kuala Lumpur discount rate.** CIDB Table 3.22 gives 5% for Kuala Lumpur. Some property-portal reporting gives 7%
  (search snippet only, not opened). The CIDB table is used because it is a dated official publication.
- **Selangor quota.** The CIDB table (from REHDA Institute) lists 50-70% by house type for developments under 10 acres.
  The Selangor exco's 2024 assembly answer gives a "minimum 30%" quota. Both can be true, since the minimum and the
  imposed rate differ by project. The assembly answer is authoritative for the state floor.
- **Ageing of unsold units.** The card's "46% over a year" contradicts the source's "46% more than 36 months". The source governs.
- **Later REHDA figures (not used in cards):** 33% of Bumiputera lots unsold three years after completion (Jun 2024
  survey, 8). The April 2025 headline figure of 77% of unsold Bumiputera units at RM300,001-RM500,000 is not
  supported by the article's own breakdown (9). Neither is used.
