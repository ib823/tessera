## ISSUE

On 18 September 2026 the Legal Affairs Division of the Prime Minister's Department (BHEUU)
announced that the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, on the advice of the 64th meeting of the Pardons Board for
the Federal Territories, had granted Najib Razak a conditional pardon in the SRC International case:
the remainder of his six-year sentence may be served under house arrest until **23 August 2028**,
**subject to payment of the RM50 million fine** and to other conditions the statement did not
disclose [S1, S2]. The RM50 million is the fine the same board cut from RM210 million on 29 January
2024, when it also halved the jail term; that decision said non-payment would add a year, to 23
August 2029 [S5, S6].

Within hours, Umno president Ahmad Zahid Hamidi announced a nationwide donation drive to pay the
fine, "carried out transparently, in an orderly manner, and in full compliance with all applicable
rules and laws" [S8]. Umno headquarters then published the mechanics: **Tabung Solidariti Najib
Razak**, a CIMB Islamic account in the name of *Pertubuhan Kebangsaan Melayu Bersatu (UMNO)*,
supervised by Umno HQ, open to "the leadership, Umno members and the whole of society" to give
"voluntarily according to their means" [S9, S10]. The party's secretary-general reported RM165,645
in the first hour [S11] and RM634,562.02 in three hours, many gifts of RM10 and RM50 [S12]. No cap,
no donor-disclosure rule, no audit arrangement and no rule for any surplus has been published.

Mainstream coverage carries the pardon, the reactions and the running total. What it does not carry
is the statute that would govern this money, because there is none. Malaysia has **no law that says
who may give a political party money, how much, or whether the public may know** [S23, S26, S27].
The three laws that touch party money do something else:

- the **Election Offences Act 1954** caps a *candidate's* spending during a campaign (RM200,000 for a
  Dewan Rakyat seat) and is silent on parties and on donations outside campaigns [S25, S27, S28];
- the **Societies Act 1966** makes every registered society, parties included, file accounts and a
  balance sheet with the Registrar within 60 days of its AGM (s. 14(1)(d)); s. 29 gives a copy of the
  annual return only to "every member or subscriber or person having an interest in its funds". The
  public is not on that list [S24];
- the **MACC Act 2009** defines "gratification" to begin with "money, donation, gift, loan" (s. 3) and
  criminalises corruptly receiving it (s. 16) [S29]. Ten days before the pardon, the minister in
  charge of the bill said in her personal capacity that "if there is no Political Financing Act, many
  politicians will be charged with corruption — easy, simple" [S15].

The gap has a dated history, and the party now passing the hat is at both ends of it. The
government's own consultative committee on political financing was **announced by Prime Minister
Najib on 14 August 2015**, weeks after the RM2.6 billion in his personal accounts became public; the
committee's report notes the concern "was also brought up when the Prime Minister confirmed that he
received a donation from Saudi Arabia in the lead up to the 13th General Election" [S18]. Its 32
recommendations (29 August 2016) included a Political Donations and Expenditure Act, a Controller,
donor identification above RM3,000 within 30 days, a ban on foreign cash and on donations from
government contractors, and confiscation of money from unknown sources [S18]. The Act was to be in
Parliament before GE14 [S20, S21]. It never was. Pakatan Harapan's 2018 manifesto opened Promise 18
with the RM2.6 billion and pledged a Political Financing Control Act whose first principle was that
"all political contributions must be from sources that can be identified" [S22]. GIACC said in
September 2019 the bill would be tabled the following month [S31]; the Sheraton Move ended the
government in February 2020 and the draft with it [S33]. Ismail Sabri's cabinet committee agreed "in
principle" in May 2022 [S34]; Anwar's Cabinet agreed "in principle" on 8 September 2023 [S13]. On 8
September 2026 Azalina Othman Said listed nine "parameters" still being fine-tuned, asked BHEUU to
collect the written stand of all ~72 registered parties, and put tabling in 2027 [S13, S14, S16].
Her own engagement data: 100 % of 1,544 participants said Malaysia needs the law; 90.3 % wanted it
passed before GE16 [S13].

The developable core is not the pardon (a royal prerogative under Article 42, with the AG and the
Federal Territories minister on the board, whose reasons are never published [S3, S4]) and not
whether the drive is lawful (nothing prohibits it, which is the point). It is that **eleven years
after a prime minister's "donation" put the question on the table, the same party can collect
RM50 million for the same man through its own registered-society account with no rule on who gives,
how much, or whether anyone will ever know**, and the government that promised the rule in 2018 has
moved its date to 2027. Anger-at-process: the statute, not the party, not the donors, and never the
pardon.

## RELEVANT LAW

- **Federal Constitution, Art. 42** — power of pardon; (5) board of AG, Chief Minister / FT minister and up to three appointees; (8) meets in the presence of the Ruler, who presides; (9) must consider the AG's written opinion. Reported via [S3], [S4]. **Primary text to be cited from the AGC's Federal Constitution reprint (GAP 6).**
- **Election Offences Act 1954 (Act 5), s. 19** — maximum election expenses: RM200,000 for a Dewan Rakyat candidate; state figure reported as RM100,000 [S20, S28] and as RM150,000 [S25] (CONTRADICTION 1). Applies to candidates and election agents; illegal practice if exceeded. **Official text not opened (GAP 1).**
- **Societies Act 1966 (Act 832), s. 14(1)(d)** — accounts of the last financial year "together with a balance sheet" to the Registrar within 60 days of the AGM; (db) description of any money received from any person "ordinarily resident outside Malaysia"; **s. 29** — copy of the annual return "free of charge to every member or subscriber or person having an interest in its funds on his application"; **s. 30** — members may inspect the books. Part IA applies to political parties [S24]. No provision for public access.
- **MACC Act 2009 (Act 694), s. 3** — "gratification" means "(a) money, donation, gift, loan, fee, reward, valuable security, property … financial benefit, or any other similar advantage …"; **s. 16** — offence of corruptly soliciting, receiving, giving or offering gratification "as an inducement to or a reward for" any act; **s. 17A** corporate liability [S29].
- **NCCPF report (29 Aug 2016)** — R4 new Political Donation and Expenditure Act; R7 Office of the Controller of Political Donations and Expenditure; R9 Parliamentary Standing Committee; R13 ban on foreign cash; R18 money from unknown sources confiscated; R20 no cap on donations; R22/R24 disclosure above RM3,000 (single or cumulative per annum) to the Controller within 30 days; R23 remove spending limits; R26 full disclosure by GE15; R28 SOE ban; R29 ban on donations by government contractors and concessionaires; R30 donor-protection mechanism [S18].
- **Buku Harapan Promise 18 (8 Mar 2018)** — Political Financing Control Act; identifiable sources; audited annual reports; RM1b asset cap; GLC ban; state funding by formula [S22].
- **Comparators** — Singapore Political Donations Act 2000: only permissible donors (citizens ≥21, Singapore-controlled companies); anonymous donations capped at S$5,000 per reporting period; donations aggregating S$10,000 recorded in the donation report [S57]. UK PPERA 2000: donations over £11,180 a year reported to the Electoral Commission (threshold since 1 Jan 2024; previously £7,500) [S58]. Indonesia Law 2/2011: non-member individuals ≤ Rp1 billion a year, corporations ≤ Rp7.5 billion [S59]. **Open each primary text before use (GAP 8).**

## RECOMMENDED LENSES

1. **Governance** — the eleven-year record: a committee announced by Najib in 2015, a report in 2016,
   a manifesto pledge in 2018, "next month" in 2019, "in principle" in 2022 and 2023, "next year" in
   2026. Five governments, one identical outcome. Carry the 100 % / 90.3 % engagement numbers.
2. **Legal** — what the three existing laws actually do: a candidate cap during campaigns (Election
   Offences Act s. 19), accounts filed but visible only to members (Societies Act ss. 14, 29), and a
   definition of gratification that starts with the word "donation" (MACC Act s. 3). The minister's
   own line that without the Act politicians "will be charged with corruption — easy, simple".
3. **Political** — the fund itself: a party inside the governing coalition collecting into its own
   registered-society account for a convicted former president, RM634,562 in three hours, no cap, no
   names, no audit rule announced; the Vibes' question of who the large donors will be. Attribute
   every characterisation; state what is unpublished as unpublished.
   (Alternative third lens: **Historical** — Promise 18 opens with the RM2.6 billion; the NCCPF report
   records the Saudi-donation confirmation as a trigger; the 2025 verdict found the Arab letters
   forged.)

Reframe candidate (causal restructuring): *The scandal is not that a party is passing the hat for a
convicted former prime minister. It is that eleven years after his RM2.6 billion "donation" started
the committee, no law says who may give a party money, how much, or whether anyone gets to know.*

Reframe candidate (question restructuring): *The question is not whether Umno may raise RM50
million. Nothing stops it. The question is why the law that would tell us who paid has been "in
principle" under three governments and is now dated 2027.*

View candidate (WhatsApp test): *A fine set by the Pardons Board will be paid through a bank account
the public cannot inspect, under a law that does not exist. The government that promised that law in
2018 now says 2027. Both facts are on the record; only one of them is a choice.*

## CONTRADICTIONS

1. **State-seat spending cap under the Election Offences Act s. 19.** Paul Low (2016) and TI-M
   (2016, quoting the Act) give RM100,000 for a state seat [S20, S25]; Focus Malaysia and a MACC web
   page give RM150,000 [S28]. The RM200,000 Dewan Rakyat figure is consistent everywhere. Use only
   the RM200,000 figure on cards unless GAP 1 is closed with the official text.
2. **Pardons Board 2024 date.** Reporting says "February 2024"; the secretariat's statement says the
   decision was taken at the 29 January 2024 meeting and announced on 2 February [S5, S6]. Write
   "January 2024" or "announced 2 February 2024".
3. **Engagement session counts.** Azalina: 18 sessions, 12 at state level, 1,544 participants [S14,
   S16]; Punitha: 1,544 participants in 12 sessions June–November 2025 [S13]. Same participants;
   different session counts. Write "1,544 participants in BHEUU's engagement sessions" and avoid the
   session number.
4. **Umno's stated reason for the 2015 committee.** Najib said it followed his 2009 pledge and
   denied a link to the RM2.6b [S19]; the committee's own report cites the Saudi-donation
   confirmation as a source of public concern (para 9) and Promise 18 states the link outright
   [S18, S22]. Report Najib's denial alongside the report's paragraph; do not write "because of".
5. **NCCPF on caps.** The report recommends *no cap* on donations (R20) and removal of spending
