# BRIEF: BUDI95 RON95 quota cut to 200 litres on 1 April 2026; the "90% unaffected" figure came without a method

## ISSUE

On 26 March 2026 the Ministry of Finance announced that the monthly BUDI95 entitlement to RON95 at RM1.99 a litre would fall from 300 to 200 litres from 1 April 2026, as a temporary measure while the West Asia conflict pushed crude above US$100 a barrel and petrol and diesel subsidies toward RM4 billion a month. MOF said "nearly 90%" of eligible users use less than 200 litres a month (average about 100 litres) and would not be affected, but published no method or sample period; fuel bought above the quota is sold at the weekly unsubsidised price. The issue argues that a targeted measure should publish the arithmetic behind its central claim. The quota was restored to 300 litres from 1 September 2026.

## CONTRADICTIONS

1. **Unsubsidised RON95 price, week of 16–22 April 2026.** The card says RM3.87; MOF's own weekly release says RM4.02 [3]. MOF is authoritative. The RM3.87 figure is correct for 26 Mar–8 Apr and 23–29 Apr [2, 3, 4].
2. **"90%" versus "nearly 90%".** MOF wrote "hampir 90%" [1]. Minor rounding on the cards.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- **Quota restored to 300 litres on 1 September 2026.** Fiscal Outlook 2027: "Following the stabilisation of the domestic energy supplies, the monthly quota for BUDI95 was restored to 300 litres, effective 1 September 2026" [6]; also The Star, 30 Aug 2026 [11]. The headline, context and hook ("RON95 stays at RM1.99 for the first 200 litres") describe a rule that no longer applies.
- **Usage data published, for 300 litres.** Budget 2027 (9 Oct 2026): "September's usage records show that only around 100 thousand users, or less than 1 per cent, reached their 300-litre BUDI95 limit" [7]. Still no distribution behind the 200-litre "90%" claim.
- **Cost context.** Fuel subsidy spending rose to RM40 billion in 2026 [6, 7].
