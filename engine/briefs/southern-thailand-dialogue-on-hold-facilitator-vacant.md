## ISSUE

Malaysia has facilitated the Thailand–BRN peace dialogue since the General Consensus on Peace
Dialogue Process was signed in Kuala Lumpur on 28 February 2013 [S32, S33]. In 2026 the process
stalled in a sequence that Malaysian coverage has reported piece by piece and never assembled:

- **22 July 2026.** Six masked assailants opened fire and threw pipe bombs at a security checkpoint
  at Ban Bukit Sami, Rangae district, Narathiwat, at about 6.45 pm, killing five paramilitary
  rangers and injuring six civilians including a three-year-old girl [S9, S15].
- **28 July 2026.** Thailand's National Intelligence Agency director Thanat Suwannanont, who also
  chairs the Thai peace dialogue panel, announced the talks scheduled for September in Malaysia were
  suspended: "There will be no negotiations in September ... The violence was too severe for us to
  accept" [S9].
- **31 July 2026.** Thailand's National Security Council said it would review the dialogue
  framework "following the temporary suspension of peace talks during the transition to a new
  facilitator" [S10]. Seventeen days earlier, on 14 July, Defence Minister Mohamed Khaled Nordin had
  told reporters that Malaysia's facilitator Mohd Rabin Basir "will continue his role" [S8].
- **22 August 2026, 8 pm to midnight.** Fifty-one coordinated incidents across 21 districts of
  Narathiwat, Yala and Pattani, per the Internal Security Operations Command's final count: arson at
  local government offices, telecom masts and a 7-Eleven, six bombings, tyre fires and caltrops on
  roads. Three civilians wounded. Narathiwat imposed a curfew under the Martial Law Act of 1914
  [S11, S12, S13, S16]. Blasts shook houses in Kampung Lubok Gong and Kampung Tersang, Rantau
  Panjang, Kelantan [S17]. Fourth Army Region commander Norathip Poinok said intelligence had warned
  of attacks on local offices "for about two months" and admitted "our community engagement is still
  not good enough" [S12, S15].
- **26 August 2026.** Inspector-General of Police Mohd Khalid Ismail said PDRM had received a
  request from Thai police and military "to search for or assist with any movement of agents or
  individuals connected with BRN" and would investigate, gather intelligence and assist [S20].
- **27 August 2026.** Khaled confirmed Rabin's tenure "ended recently", that his "contract has also
  expired", and that the Armed Forces had submitted several names to the Prime Minister; a
  facilitator would be appointed "soon" [S21, S22, S23].
- **7 September 2026.** Thai Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sihasak Phuangketkeow, in
  Kuala Lumpur, said the choice of facilitator "was ultimately Malaysia's decision" and that "if the
  situation remains like this, with violence occurring, it will be difficult to hold talks" [S26].
- **14 September 2026.** BRN negotiation-delegation spokesman Nikmatullah Seri, interviewed by AFP
  in a Kuala Lumpur hotel, said the talks "had been hampered by the absence of a Malaysian mediator"
  and that the BRN could go to "back-channel negotiations" with Thailand in October. Khaled, in a
  statement to AFP, said the appointment of a new mediator was "under consideration" [S30].
- **17 September 2026.** Chief of Army Azhan Md Othman said the Army was using drones to monitor
  hard-to-reach stretches of the Thai border "particularly in response to the unrest in southern
  Thailand": "We will ensure that no elements are able to breach our border. There will be no
  compromise" [S29].

The gap the headline misses: every Malaysian security instrument on this border is switched on
(Army drones, GOF brigades, PDRM tracing BRN elements at Thai request, a fence-coordination deal),
while the one diplomatic instrument Malaysia alone controls, the facilitator's chair, has been
vacant for at least three weeks by the government's own account and, on the Thai NSC's wording,
since late July. Bangkok says it is Malaysia's call. The BRN says the empty chair is what stalled
the talks. Putrajaya has said "soon" (27 August) and "under consideration" (18 September) and
named no date.

**What this issue does NOT claim.** It does not attribute the 22 July or 22 August attacks to the
BRN (ACLED: "too early to attribute"; no group claimed responsibility; Nikmatullah "neither
confirmed nor denied") [S12, S16, S30]. It does not say Malaysia harbours militants: the BRN
delegation in KL is the negotiating party in a process Thailand asked Malaysia to host, and the
IGP's tracing assistance is at Thai request. It does not say the facilitator vacancy caused the
suspension: Thanat's stated reason was the 22 July attack; the NSC's 31 July wording adds the
transition. Both reasons run on the cards, attributed.

## RELEVANT LAW

- **General Consensus on Peace Dialogue Process (28 Feb 2013)** — the framework under which
  Malaysia "act[s] as facilitator"; roughly 200 words; parties are the Thai NSC and "people who have
  different opinions and ideologies than the state" [S32, S33]. No Malaysian statute governs the
  facilitator role; it is an executive appointment announced by MKN [S1].
- **Thailand: Martial Law Act B.E. 2457 (1914), ss 6, 8, 9, 11** — invoked for the Narathiwat
  curfew 22–23 Aug [S11]; martial law in 13 Narathiwat districts after 22 Jul [S9]. Emergency
  Decree (2005) remains in force in the region [S3, S30].
- **Thailand: Constitution s.1** — "Thailand is one and indivisible Kingdom"; the ceiling on any
  "end state" [S35]. Background only.
- **Malaysia–Thailand bilateral mechanisms** — General Border Committee (GBC, 57th meeting hosted
  by Malaysia in 2026), Regional Border Committee (RBC, 1–3 Sep Hat Yai), Border Security
  Management Committee (twice yearly) [S7, S19, S39].

## CONTRADICTIONS

1. **Incident count, 22 Aug.** The Nation's first report: 25 incidents across 13 districts [S11].
   Forward Operations Coordination Centre 4 at 9.42 pm: 34 [S13]. ISOC's final count, carried by
   AFP, Reuters and ACLED: 51 across 21 districts [S12, S16]. Bernama/Khaled: "more than 50"
   [S21]. One search summary cited 78 across four provinces; no primary source found; discarded.
   **Authoritative: 51 (ISOC final).** Cards say "51 incidents" attributed to ISOC.
2. **Who died on 22 Jul.** Bernama and The Nation: five "rangers" / "paramilitary rangers" [S9,
   S15]; AFP: "five soldiers" [S30]; Reuters/SCMP: "five security personnel". **Use "five
   rangers"** (Thai official description of thahan phran). The operator's shorthand "five
   soldiers" is acceptable in the hook only if the fact card corrects to rangers; prefer rangers
   throughout.
3. **Why the talks were suspended.** Thanat, 28 Jul: the attack [S9]. Thai NSC, 31 Jul: suspension
   "during the transition to a new facilitator" [S10]. AFP, 18 Sep, Thai foreign ministry: the
   soldiers' deaths put dialogue "on hold" [S30]. **Both reasons are official; run both,
   attributed.** Do not rank them.
4. **Facilitator status in July.** Khaled, 14 Jul: Rabin "will continue his role" [S8]. Thai NSC,
   31 Jul: "transition to a new facilitator" [S10]. Khaled, 27 Aug: contract "expired", tenure
   "ended recently" [S21, S22]. **Authoritative on the vacancy: 27 Aug (the minister's own
   confirmation).** The 14 Jul line is quoted as what was said then, not as error.
5. **Thanut's appointment month.** Thai PBS World 23 Apr 2026 (NSC announcement) [S4]; Al Jazeera
   "in April" [S12]; Media Selangor "in May" [S6]. **Authoritative: 23 April (announcement date).**
6. **Cumulative deaths.** Deep South Watch: "more than 7,000" (AFP) [S30], 7,790 Jan 2004–Dec 2025
   (Nation citing DSW) [S13], "more than 7,800" (Reuters/SCMP) ; SBPAC certified: 5,999 to 26 Dec
   2025 [S31]. Different datasets (monitoring vs compensation-certified). **Cards use one figure
   with its dataset named**, preferably SBPAC's 5,999 as the conservative certified count, or DSW's
   "more than 7,000" with "monitoring group Deep South Watch". Never mix.
7. **Rabin's appointment date.** An earlier search summary gave "5 July 2025". Bernama's MKN
   statement of 5 July 2024: effective 1 July 2024 [S1]; Media Selangor: "appointed on July 1, 2024"
   [S6]. **Authoritative: 1 July 2024.**
8. **Curfew end time.** The Nation/Star: 6 am [S11]; Bernama: 5 am [S20, S21]. Minor; cards avoid
   the end time.
