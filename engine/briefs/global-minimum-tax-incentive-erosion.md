## ISSUE
For four decades Malaysia competed for foreign direct investment by waiving corporate tax: pioneer status,
the Investment Tax Allowance, and reinvestment allowances cut effective rates far below the 24% headline,
sometimes close to zero. The OECD's Pillar Two Global Minimum Tax (GMT) sets a 15% minimum effective rate
on large multinational groups and took effect in Malaysia for financial years beginning 1 January 2025.
The under-covered consequence: when a tax holiday pulls a firm's Malaysian effective rate below 15%, a
"top-up" tax is now owed somewhere. If Malaysia does not collect it, the firm's home country can. The
incentive stops being an incentive. Malaysia adopted a domestic top-up tax (QDMTT) to keep that revenue,
and launched a New Incentive Framework (NIF, effective 1 March 2026) to pivot from tax holidays to
outcome-based grants. A 40-year industrial-policy tool has been quietly neutralised, with little public debate.

## VERIFICATION (load-bearing facts)
- **GMT effective for FY beginning 1 Jan 2025; 15% ETR; €750M consolidated-revenue threshold.** SOLID.
  MIDA GMT page (confirmed by direct fetch 2026-06-20); HASIL Guidelines on the Implementation of GMT in
  Malaysia (21 Dec 2024); Crowe Malaysia. Multiple-source corroboration including the regulator.
- **Mechanism: QDMTT + IIR both from 1 Jan 2025.** SOLID. MIDA (direct fetch): "Qualified Domestic Minimum
  Top-up Tax (QDMTT) and Income Inclusion Rule (IIR) starting from the same date." Malaysia added to the
  OECD list with Qualified DMTT + QDMTT Safe Harbour status as of 1 Jan 2025 (Crowe; oecdpillars.com).
- **Statutory basis: Finance Act (No. 2) 2023, gazetted 29 Dec 2023** (introduced the Multinational Top-up
  Tax / GloBE rules into the Income Tax framework). SOLID per Crowe; confirm the exact gazette reference and
  the QDMTT provisions before publish.
- **New Incentive Framework (NIF): manufacturing effective 1 March 2026; services Q2 2026; new manufacturing
  applications under the old regime not accepted after 28 Feb 2026; outcome-based via the National Investment
  Aspirations (NIA) scorecard, delivered as a Special Tax Rate (STR) or Investment Tax Allowance (ITA).**
  SOLID. MITI NIF page; MIDA media release; KPMG; US-ASEAN Business Council.
- **"Government is reviewing the tax incentive framework to align with international tax policy."** SOLID,
  quoted on the MIDA GMT page (direct fetch).

## CLAIMS DROPPED / SOFTENED FOR ACCURACY
- **No GMT revenue estimate is published in the cards.** Neither MIDA, HASIL, nor the searched budget
  coverage gave a verified ringgit figure for expected QDMTT collections. The "tax expenditure" / forgone-
  revenue total from incentives is also not stated as a number. The cards rest on the *mechanism* (top-up
  owed somewhere; QDMTT keeps it onshore), which is fully sourced, not on an unverified revenue number.
- "Near zero" effective rates under pioneer status: framed as "as low as near zero" / "deep below the
  headline rate," not a precise universal claim. Pioneer status grants up to 70-100% income-tax exemption
  depending on tier; effective rates vary by firm. Cards avoid asserting a single number.
- Penang/semiconductor framing: stated as the cluster "built substantially on" tax incentives, an accurate
  characterisation of Malaysia's electronics FDI history, not a claim that incentives were the only factor.

## KEY STATISTICS
- Minimum effective tax rate: **15%**. Scope threshold: **€750 million** consolidated group revenue in ≥2 of
  the 4 preceding fiscal years. Effective in Malaysia: **FY beginning on/after 1 Jan 2025**.
- Malaysia's headline corporate tax rate: **24%** (context; the gap between 24% and near-zero incentive
  rates is what GMT compresses toward 15%).
- NIF dates: manufacturing **1 March 2026**; old-regime manufacturing applications cut off **28 Feb 2026**.
