# Brief — Issue 2010: The uncapped pension bill and the thinning support ratio

## ISSUE
Malaysia's fiscal debate fixates on the 65% statutory debt ceiling. The civil-service pension bill sits
entirely outside that ceiling and outside any cap. It is a defined-benefit promise to nearly one million
pensioners, paid pay-as-you-go from each year's revenue. Retirement charges are projected at RM42.8 billion
in 2026 and, on the government's own projection, reach roughly RM120 billion a year by 2040. The unfunded
actuarial liability behind the promise is estimated between RM483 billion and RM1.3 trillion, with no single
audited figure. Meanwhile the demographic base funding it is thinning: the support ratio falls from about
eight working-age adults per person aged 65+ in 2022 toward fewer than five by 2040, and 3.45 million
informal workers will reach old age largely outside the EPF. Media reports these as three separate stories.

## VERIFICATION (load-bearing facts)
- **Retirement charges RM42.8B (2026, projected), up 6.8% from revised RM40.06B (2025); fourth-largest
  operating-expenditure item.** SOLID. The Edge (Budget 2026 coverage); MOF Fiscal Outlook 2026. Corroborated.
- **Pension payments projected ~RM120B/year by 2040, from ~RM30.5B currently.** SOLID. Reported from the
  government's own projection (Malay Mail / Dewan Rakyat; matches the research doc's OFFICIAL PROJ of RM120B
  for 2040 and RM46.4B for 2030). Annual *expenditure* projection, distinct from the actuarial *stock*.
- **Pensioners nearing 1 million (2025).** SOLID. Multiple outlets; research doc series 859,422 (2020) →
  931,707 (2023) [Dewan Rakyat]. Confirm the latest count against a parliamentary answer or KWAP before publish.
- **Unfunded actuarial liability RM483B (2017) / ~RM800B / RM1.3 trillion — no single audited figure.**
  CARRY AS RANGE. Different methods, none audited (research doc §C contradiction register). Cards present it
  as a range explicitly and do NOT pick one number.
- **Support ratio ~7.9 (2022) → ~4.6 (2040); old-age dependency 12.6 (2022) → 21.7 (2040).** OFFICIAL PROJ
  (DOSM Population Projections). Cards state "about eight ... fewer than five." Confirm the exact 2040 figure
  and the working-age definition (DOSM uses 65+ per 100 aged 20-59) against the DOSM projection PDF.
- **65+ population 2.6M = 7.7% (2024); crossed the 7% "ageing" threshold in 2021; "aged nation" (14%)
  projected ~2048 (band 2044-2048).** SOLID per DOSM; carry the 2044-2048 band (research doc §C).
- **3.45 million informal workers (2023) largely outside EPF/SOCSO.** From DOSM Informal Sector Survey 2023
  (via research doc). Confirm against the DOSM release before publish.
- **A new (defined-contribution) pension scheme for NEW civil servants has been announced.** SOLID (DPM;
  Asia Asset Management). Context for the view card: it addresses the future intake, not the existing liability.

## CLAIMS DROPPED / SOFTENED FOR ACCURACY
- **Annual flow vs actuarial stock kept strictly distinct.** RM42.8B (2026) and ~RM120B (2040) are annual
  *spend*; RM483B-RM1.3T is the *liability stock*. A search summary conflated "liabilities ... RM46.36B by
  2030" — that RM46.36B is the 2030 annual-spend projection, NOT the stock. Cards never merge the two.
- Did NOT reuse the "emoluments + pensions ~45% of opex" framing as a card claim, to avoid colliding/
  conflating with existing issues on the salary share (2001 health-budget salaries; 1192 civil-service 82%
  of opex). This issue is about pensions specifically, not the wage bill.
- No claim that pensions "caused" the deficit; framed as an uncapped, growing, unfunded obligation.

## KEY STATISTICS
- Pension/retirement charges: RM42.8B (2026 proj), RM40.06B (2025 rev), ~RM11B (2010); ~RM120B/yr by 2040 (proj).
- Pensioners: ~931,707 (2023), nearing 1,000,000 (2025).
- Unfunded actuarial liability: RM483B (2017) / ~RM800B / RM1.3T — range, no audited figure.
- Support ratio: ~7.9 (2022) → ~4.6 (2040). Old-age dependency: 12.6 (2022) → 21.7 (2040).
- 65+: 7.7% (2024); 7% crossed 2021; 14% ~2048. Informal workers: 3.45M (2023).
- Statutory debt ceiling: 65% of GDP (for context — the capped number media debates).
