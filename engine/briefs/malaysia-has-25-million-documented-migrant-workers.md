# BRIEF: Malaysia's registered foreign workforce and its US Trafficking in Persons record

**Issue ID:** 1267
**Slug:** malaysia-has-25-million-documented-migrant-workers
**Source date:** 2026-04-08
**Backfill date:** 2026-11-08
**Brief status:** Source-list backfill. Issue cards unchanged; findings below are for the editor.

## ISSUE

The Ministry of Finance's Economic Outlook 2025 counted about 2.5 million registered low-skilled foreign workers at end-August 2024: 37.8% from Bangladesh, 23.7% from Indonesia and 16.7% from Nepal [1]. By 15 Oct 2025, before this issue's source date, the Human Resources Ministry told the Dewan Rakyat the figure had fallen 13% to 2.132 million [2]. The US 2025 Trafficking in Persons (TIP) Report kept Malaysia on Tier 2 [4]. It found fewer convictions (26, against 54) and no labour-trafficking victims identified or prosecutions in palm oil or disposable gloves [4]. Malaysia was on Tier 3 in 2014 [5] and again in 2021–2022 [6, 10].

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **Size of the foreign workforce.**
  - MOF, end-August 2024: about 2.5 million registered low-skilled foreign workers [1].
  - Immigration Department via the HR Ministry, Hansard 3 Nov 2025: 2,368,422 recorded at 31 Dec 2024, and 2.132 million active at 15 Oct 2025 [2].
  - The Star reports the same 15 Oct 2025 figure as 2,132,578 [3].
  - **Judged authoritative for "now" at the source date:** the October 2025 parliamentary figure [2], the most recent official count. The cards use the older MOF figure in the present tense.
- **Start date of the Employment Act coverage.** The cards say 1 Jan 2023. The AGC reprint lists the First Schedule order P.U. (A) 262/2022 in force from 1 Sep 2022, and Act A1651 from 1 Jan 2023 [7]. **Judged authoritative:** the reprint's amendment list.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- **9 Oct 2026:** The 2026 TIP Report kept Malaysia on Tier 2 for the third consecutive year [9, 10]. Malaysiakini reports convictions rose to 36 from 26 and no employment agency was prosecuted under the Private Employment Agencies Act 1981 [10]. The cards' 2025 references stay accurate as history. Hook and context should cite the 2026 report as the latest, and the "fewer convictions" trend has reversed.
