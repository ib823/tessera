## ISSUE

Three platform investigations and one journalism consortium have documented, since 2019, a market
in which fake accounts, fake local news pages and hacked inboxes are sold to whoever pays. The
documented sellers are private firms, three of them Israeli and one British; the documented buyers
are governments, ministries, campaigns and companies on four continents, and one of the documented
operations was traced by Meta to the Royal Malaysia Police.

- **Archimedes Group (Tel Aviv), May 2019.** Facebook removed 265 accounts, Pages, Groups and
  events that "represented themselves as locals, including local news organizations, and published
  allegedly leaked information about politicians", with about US$812,000 in ads paid in Brazilian
  reais, shekels and dollars, and 2.8 million followers [S1]. Targets: Nigeria, Senegal, Togo,
  Angola, Niger, Tunisia, "along with some activity in Latin America and Southeast Asia" [S1]. AP,
  quoting the Atlantic Council's DFRLab: "The most significant audience engagement was generated in
  Malaysia, which has a vast media market and held a general election last year" [S2].
- **Royal Malaysia Police troll farm, August 2022.** Meta removed 596 Facebook accounts, 180 Pages,
  11 Groups and 72 Instagram accounts that "posted memes in Malay in support of the current
  government coalition, with claims of corruption among its critics", managed Pages "posing as
  independent news entities", "promoted police while criticizing the opposition", and took breaks
  for lunch. Followers: about 427,000. Ad spend: about US$6,000, mostly in ringgit. "Our
  investigation found links to the Royal Malaysia Police" [S3]. The police secretary denied the
  link on 6 August 2022 and said the force was "gathering further information" [S5]. No outcome
  has been published in four years [S5, S6]. The same Meta report removed 259 accounts linked to
  Mind Force, a PR firm in Israel, targeting Angola, Nigeria and Gaza [S3].
- **Team Jorge (Demoman International), February 2023.** A 30-outlet consortium led by Forbidden
  Stories filmed Tal Hanan telling undercover reporters "[We have completed] 33 presidential-level
  campaigns, 27 of which were successful", demonstrating Aims, software controlling more than
  30,000 fake profiles, and hacking a Telegram account "belonging to a person in Indonesia" and the
  Gmail of a Kenyan campaign aide. His services were offered to "intelligence agencies, political
  campaigns and private companies". Demoman is registered on an Israeli Ministry of Defence
  export-promotion site. Hanan: "I deny any wrongdoing." The Guardian: "It was not possible to
  verify all of Team Jorge's claims" [S7, S8].
- **Stoic (Tel Aviv), May–June 2024.** Meta removed 510 Facebook accounts, 11 Pages, one Group and
  32 Instagram accounts that "posed as locals ... including as Jewish students, African Americans
  and 'concerned' citizens", commented under US lawmakers' Pages, bought engagement from Vietnam,
  and used likely AI-generated text; "we found links to STOIC" [S9]. OpenAI terminated the same
  operation's accounts ("Zero Zeno") [S10]. The New York Times reported Israel's Ministry of
  Diaspora Affairs paid about US$2 million for it; the ministry denied involvement [S11].
- **SCL / Cambridge Analytica, 2013 and 2018.** Cambridge Analytica's website listed a "targeted
  messaging campaign" for BN in Kedah at GE13. In March 2018 the Prime Minister's Office said
  neither firm was ever "contracted, employed or paid in any way by Barisan Nasional, the Prime
  Minister's Office or any part of the Government of Malaysia" and that SCL's representative
  "reported directly to Mukhriz Mahathir"; Mukhriz denied using the firm; SCL's Southeast Asia head
  Azrin Zizal said he advised Mukhriz "personally" through GE13 [S12, S13, S14].

Mainstream coverage of each episode ran for a news cycle in the country where it broke. What it
does not carry, and what T4A can, is the pattern: the product is the same in every case (fake
locals, fake news pages, leaked-document claims), the sellers are companies not states, the buyers
are whoever pays, and Malaysia appears on both sides of the ledger, once as the largest audience of
an Israeli firm's fake pages and once as the home of a troll farm Meta traced to its own police.
The domestic gap is the response: the police denial of 2022 has never been followed by a finding,
Malaysia's Online Safety Act 2025 (in force 1 January 2026, codes from 1 June 2026) regulates
harmful content, deepfake labelling and advertiser verification but has no provision on
coordinated inauthentic behaviour or foreign interference as such [S15, S16], while Singapore used
its 2021 foreign-interference law for the first time in July 2024 to restrict 95 accounts across
five platforms [S17].

Anger-at-process: the unanswered 2022 finding and the statute that does not name the behaviour.
Not anger at any state. The Israeli firms are described by what Meta, OpenAI and the consortium
documented; the Israeli government appears once, as the reported client of Stoic, with its denial.
