# BRIEF — Malaysian rules bar bulk exports of blended palm oil unless every shipping paper states the mix. A complaint says one Port Klang cargo's papers did not, and MPOB has said nothing in public since 22 October.

## ISSUE

**Thesis after research: the process question holds; several parts of the working thesis were overstated.**

**What the complaint says (one party's account, unproven).** Maritime Network Sdn Bhd, the port agent appointed by the vessel's owner for the Port Klang call, alleges that on 30 September 2026 Indonesian refined, bleached and deodorised (RBD) palm olein was mixed with Malaysian crude palm olein aboard the tanker MT Eunice at Port Klang; that the bill of lading was then changed so the whole cargo was declared as crude palm olein; and that the shipper's name was changed to a Malaysian company before the cargo left for Egypt [S16a, S7c]. Its chief executive, Datuk Seri Jeyenderan Ramasamy, lodged a complaint with MPOB on 19 October [S16a, S17a]. His firm's lawyers sent Olam Global Agri Pte Ltd a letter of demand dated 29 October; that letter says the vessel loaded "about 35,432 tonnes of palm oil products at Dumai, Indonesia" and then "a further 4,999.715 tonnes of Malaysian crude palm olein at Port Klang" for Adabiya, Egypt, and itself "acknowledged that the precise nature and extent of any commingling still needed documentary verification" (as reported) [S15]. No regulator, court or buyer has found that anything unlawful happened. Olam has made no public statement that we could find; its position is known only through the complainant's letter [S15]. Mewah Oils Sdn Bhd is named in that letter only as the seller of the Malaysian parcel; no one has alleged wrongdoing by Mewah [S15].

**Corrections to the working thesis.**
1. *"The shipping papers were changed to declare the whole cargo as Malaysian crude palm olein."* Overstated. The complaint says the papers declared the whole cargo as **crude palm olein** and named a **Malaysian shipper** [S16a, S7c]. No source says any document declared **Malaysian origin**, and no source says whether a certificate of origin was issued, or by whom (GAP 3).
2. *"MPOB confirmed an investigation."* Contested. The Sun reports MPOB's letter of 22 October, signed by Enforcement Unit head Wan Yasin Wan Daud, "confirmed" an investigation [S16a, S7c]. Utusan reports the same letter "merekodkan aduan berkenaan" (recorded the complaint), with a complaint-receipt record attached, and that the mixing and document claims "tidak disahkan menerusi surat rasmi MPOB" [S17a]. Oriental Daily reports the letter confirmed the complaint was formally recorded [S17b]. The letter is unpublished. Safe wording: **MPOB acknowledged the complaint in writing on 22 October; the complainant says it has heard nothing since** (CONTRADICTION 1).
3. *Lead: "Olam's counsel disputes prior notification/consent."* Reversed. Per the Malay Mail report of the letter of demand, **Olam allegedly asserted** that Maritime Network had prior knowledge of and consented to the cargo arrangements; **Maritime Network disputes** that, saying "no contemporaneous written notification or documentary evidence of such consent had been produced" [S15]. Maritime Network also "signed the bills of lading on behalf of the vessel's master" as port agent [S15].
4. *Blending.* Mixing foreign and Malaysian palm oil is **not itself unlawful**. MPOB (Quality) Regulations 2005, reg. 12, bars selling, exporting or consigning a "blended oil palm product" in bulk (over 25 kg) **unless** the buyer asked for it in writing, the request is in the contract, and "all document of sales, delivery order, shipping documents, bill of lading and customs declaration shall indicate the percentage, ratio, weight or volume of ingredients and compositions of the blend" [S1, reg. 12(3)]. The legal question is therefore **declaration**, not mixing. Whether reg. 12 even reaches on-board commingling of an in-transit foreign cargo with an already-loaded Malaysian parcel is an open legal question no authority has answered (WEAKENS 3).
5. *EUDR.* The Egypt cargo is outside the EU Deforestation Regulation, which governs placing on, making available on, and export from the **Union** market [S8, Art. 2(16), (18), (37), Art. 3]. No MPOB, ministry, MPOC or EU source links this case or the "cocktail" claims to EUDR; only the complainant does [S19a]. Anonymous industry sources link the claims to MSPO's credibility, not to EUDR [S16a, S18]. The ministry's spokesperson "declined to comment when asked about the allegations" [S18].
6. *MSPO as an origin guarantee.* MSPO's own scheme document allows **mass balance**: products labelled under it may mix certified and non-certified palm oil, down to a 30% certified-content claim [S13, PDF pp. 48 to 49]. MSPO is a sustainability-certification scheme, not an origin-segregation guarantee.

**What survives: the process question.** The law gives MPOB a full paper trail and strong powers at the port. Exporters must file a "complete, true and accurate" declaration (Form MPOB Q1) at least two days before loading; importers file Form MPOB Q3 before unloading [S1, regs. 4, 6]. Where product "is suspected to be contaminated or blended, the authorized officer may take samples in any manner as he deems fit" [S1, reg. 9(3)]. Officers may stop and examine conveyances, which include ships, and customs officers are themselves "authorized officers" under the Act [S3a, ss. 2, 49]. But the same Act forbids disclosure of information "obtained under this Act" "except for the purposes of this Act" [S3a, s. 66(1)], lets the Director-General compound a **prescribed** offence before any prosecution, after which "no prosecution shall thereafter be instituted" [S3a, s. 71], and requires the Public Prosecutor's written consent to prosecute [S3a, s. 72(1)]. The 2005 compounding schedule lists Act s. 67 (untrue or misleading declarations) and Licensing reg. 43(1) (false entries) as compoundable, but **not** any offence under the Quality Regulations, so a reg. 12 breach is not compoundable on that schedule and would need prosecution [S2c]. No statutory duty on MPOB to publish investigation outcomes was found in Act 582 [S3a]. MPOB's Enforcement Unit page says nothing about publishing outcomes [S7a]. A declaration case can therefore be settled by compound and never reach a court record; a reg. 12 case can sit unannounced until a charge is filed.

**Developable core (anger at process, not at any company).** Malaysian law already tells exporters to write the composition of any bulk palm oil blend on the bill of lading and customs declaration, and gives MPOB power to sample a suspected blend at the port. The same law keeps MPOB's file behind a secrecy clause and lets an untrue-declaration case end in a compound. One complaint about one Port Klang cargo has sat for four weeks with only a written acknowledgement [S16a, S17a]; meanwhile palm oil imports tripled to 764,863 tonnes in 2025 [S6] and no source found publishes what share is blended and re-exported (GAP 6). The missing public answer is the process failure.

## RELEVANT LAW

**MPOB (Quality) Regulations 2005, P.U.(A) 518/2005**, made under Act 582 s. 78(1)(n); in force 1 Jan 2006 [S1]. FAOLEX mirror of the gazetted English text; no amendment found (GAP 8).
- **reg. 2(1):** "'blended oil palm product' means oil palm products produced from a process of mixing which includes putting together or reconstituting different products".
- **reg. 4(1):** "No person shall export any oil palm products unless he has made a complete, true and accurate declaration of the export to the Board in Form MPOB Q1 ... not less than two days before the date of commencement of loading". Penalty reg. 4(2): fine up to RM200,000, prison up to two years, or both.
- **reg. 6(1):** same duty for imports, Form MPOB Q3, two days before unloading.
- **reg. 9(1) to (3):** samples on request; "(3) Where any oil palm product is suspected to be contaminated or blended, the authorized officer may take samples in any manner as he deems fit."
- **reg. 12 ("Conditional prohibition on the selling, exporting or consigning of blended oil palm product"):** "(1) No person shall sell, export or consign blended oil palm product in bulk or packages exceeding twenty-five kilogrammes in weight." Penalty (2): up to RM200,000, two years, or both. "(3) Subregulation (1) shall not apply ... where— (a) such weight of the blended oil palm product is specifically requested in writing by the purchaser; (b) such request is specified in the contract of sale ...; and (c) all document of sales, delivery order, shipping documents, bill of lading and customs declaration shall indicate the percentage, ratio, weight or volume of ingredients and compositions of the blend."
- **reg. 13(1):** no sale, export or consignment of product that "does not conform to the type and quality ... specified in the contract of sale".
- **reg. 16:** "For the purpose of export, oil palm products shall be presumed to be taken or caused to be taken out of Malaysia if the oil palm products have been loaded on to a conveyance."

**MPOB (Compounding of Offences) Regulations 2005, P.U.(A) 519/2005**, in force 1 Jan 2006 [S2c].
- **reg. 2:** "The offences under the Act and regulations that are specified in the Schedule may be compounded by the Director-General under subsection 71(1) of the Act."
- **Schedule:** Act 582 "Section 67 and subsection 91(3)"; Licensing Regulations "Subregulations 15(8), 21(2), 22(3), 23(2), 28(5), 36(3), 37(4), 42(1), 43(1) and 44(4)"; Registration of Contracts Regulations 3(6), 4(5), 5(3). **No Quality Regulations offence is listed** (so not reg. 4 or reg. 12), and Licensing reg. 41 (false particulars) is not listed. Later amendments not found (GAP 8).

**MPOB (Licensing) Regulations 2005, P.U.(A) 516/2005** [S2a]; amended by P.U.(A) 68/2011 (substitutes reg. 3, forms) [S2b].
- **reg. 2:** "premises" includes a "conveyance".
- **reg. 5(1)(i):** licence needed to "export or import ... palm oil"; penalty up to RM200,000, three years, or both (5(3)).
- **reg. 28:** licensees keep records of "import, export" and stock for at least two years and produce them on demand.
- **reg. 41:** "Any person who supplies, sells or export any oil palm product which he knows to be false in particulars or different from what it is commits an offence"; up to RM250,000, three years, or both.
- **reg. 43(1)(c):** false entries in records or certificates; up to RM100,000, two years, or both.
- **reg. 44:** Director-General may require any person in the industry to furnish documents.
- **reg. 47:** products "deemed to be exported if they have been loaded on to a conveyance".

**Malaysian Palm Oil Board Act 1998 (Act 582)** [S3a]; Royal Assent 25 Jun 1998 [S3b]; no amending Act listed in the AGC amendment list for 2011 to 2026 [S3c].
- **s. 2:** "authorized officer" includes "an officer of customs"; "conveyance" includes "vessel, ship".
- **s. 3(2)(a):** objective "to promote and develop the oil palm industry of Malaysia". **s. 4(d):** "to regulate ... export and import of oil palm products".
- **s. 49:** power to stop and search conveyances.
- **s. 66(1):** "Except for the purposes of this Act, no person who has access to any record, book, register, correspondence, information, document or other material obtained under this Act shall disclose such ... to any other person." Penalty up to RM100,000, two years, or both (s. 66(2)).
- **s. 67:** untrue, inaccurate or misleading declarations; up to RM100,000, two years, or both.
- **s. 71(1) to (3):** Director-General may compound an offence "prescribed of to be a compoundable offence by regulations" for up to 50% of the maximum fine, before prosecution; once compounded, "no prosecution shall thereafter be instituted". Prescribed list: [S2c].
- **s. 72(1):** "No prosecution ... shall be instituted without the written consent of the Public Prosecutor."
- **s. 76:** protection of informers.
- **Not found:** any duty to publish investigation or compound outcomes.

**Customs Act 1967 (Act 235)**, 2011 edition [S4].
- **s. 2, "import":** "goods bona fide in transit, including goods for transhipment, shall not, for the purpose of levy of customs duties, be deemed to be imported unless they are or become uncustomed goods".
- **s. 133(1)(a), (c), (d):** declarations "untrue or incorrect in any particular"; counterfeited or falsified documents; fraudulent alteration. 2011 penalty: up to RM500,000, five years, or both. Current penalty not confirmed (CONTRADICTION 9). Cited by Dr Madzli as a possible route [S20a].

**Origin certification (Malaysia).** MITI: ordinary non-preferential certificates of origin "are issued by Chambers and Associations authorized by Ministry of International Trade & Industry"; "Product which meets rules of origin: Manufactured in Malaysia and used 100% local materials; or Manufactured in Malaysia through transformation process which alters tariff code classification at six (6) digit level; or Contains at least 25% local materials." Chambers must "Check and verify the information in the application form with attached supporting documents" [S5]. Whether a palm oil blend qualifies under the third limb, and whether any certificate was issued for this cargo, is unknown (GAP 3).

**Codex (as cited by the complainant).** The General Standard for the Labelling of Prepackaged Foods s. 4.5.1: origin "shall be declared if its omission would mislead or deceive the consumer"; s. 4.5.2: processing "which changes its nature" moves origin. **Scope (s. 1): "all prepackaged foods to be offered as such to the consumer or for catering purposes"** [S14]. A bulk tanker cargo is not prepackaged food; the Codex point does not map directly onto this cargo.

**EUDR (context; not applicable to the Egypt cargo).**
- Reg. (EU) 2023/1115 Art. 3: relevant products "shall not be placed or made available on the market or exported" unless deforestation-free, legally produced and covered by a due diligence statement [S8]. "Placing on the market" means first making available "on the Union market" (Art. 2(16)); "export" means the EU customs export procedure (Art. 2(37)) [S8].
- Art. 9(1)(c), (d): operators must hold "the country of production" and "the geolocation of all plots of land where the relevant commodities ... were produced"; "where a relevant product contains or has been made with relevant commodities produced on different plots of land, the geolocation of all different plots of land shall be included" [S8]. A blend with oil from unidentified plots could not meet this for the EU market.
- Reg. (EU) 2025/2650, new Art. 38(2): Arts 3 to 13, 16 to 24, 26, 31 and 32 "shall apply from 30 December 2026"; Art. 38(3): micro and small operators established by 31 Dec 2024 from 30 June 2027. Simplification review due "By 30 April 2026" [S9].
- Impl. Reg. (EU) 2025/1093 Art. 1(2): "A standard level shall be maintained for all countries not listed in the Annex." Malaysia and Indonesia are not listed; Egypt is listed low risk [S10].
- Del. Reg. (EU) 2026/2102: "ex 1511 Palm oil and its fractions, whether or not refined, but not chemically modified" stays in Annex I [S11b].

**MSPO (scheme rules, not law).** MSPO Certification Scheme Document: traceability models Segregation and Mass Balance; "Downgrading can only be done in the following order: Segregation -> Mass Balance -> Non MSPO Certified Palm Product"; "All sales of MSPO certified palm oil products shall be announced in MSPO Trace" [S13, PDF p. 45]. Claims: Segregation 100%; Mass Balance 70% (">70%"); Mass Balance 30% (">30%", claim only, "use of the MSPO logo is not permitted") [S13, PDF pp. 48 to 49].

## WHAT WEAKENS THE THESIS

1. **One side of a commercial dispute.** Every fact about the cargo comes from Maritime Network, which signed the bills of lading as port agent and now seeks an indemnity from Olam [S15]. Its own letter says the extent of any commingling "still needed documentary verification" and that it "was not making a final determination on whether any regulatory breach had occurred" [S15]. The complaint could be a liability manoeuvre as much as a whistle-blow.
2. **Blending is lawful if declared.** Reg. 12(3) expressly permits bulk blends with a buyer's written request, a contract term and the composition on every document [S1]. If those conditions were met, there is no offence. The buyer's (Egypt-side) contract is unknown.
3. **Reg. 12 may not reach this transfer.** Under reg. 16 (Quality) and reg. 47 (Licensing), product is "presumed"/"deemed" exported once loaded on a conveyance [S1, S2a]. If the Malaysian parcel was exported on loading and then mixed aboard with an Indonesian cargo that was in transit (not "imported" for duty purposes under Customs Act s. 2 [S4]), whether any person "export[ed] ... blended oil palm product" from Malaysia is an open legal question. No authority has addressed it. (Legal analysis by FORA; flag to Stage 3, do not state as fact.)
4. **Origin rules may allow a Malaysian certificate for a blend.** MITI's ordinary NPCO criteria include "Contains at least 25% local materials" [S5]. A blend with enough Malaysian content might lawfully carry a Malaysian certificate; no source says any certificate was sought or issued for this cargo (GAP 3).
5. **MPOB's silence may be required or routine, not neglect.** s. 66 bars disclosure of information obtained under the Act "except for the purposes of this Act"; s. 71 compounding is lawful and ends the matter; prosecution needs the Public Prosecutor's consent [S3a]. And because reg. 12 is not on the 2005 compoundable list [S2c], a proven reg. 12 breach would have to be prosecuted, which would create a public court record; "it can all be buried by compound" is too strong. Four weeks is short for a cross-border cargo investigation. The 2004 precedent: the ministry fined an exporter of "adulterated edible palm oil" without the visible report naming it [S24a], so closed-door enforcement is longstanding, not specific to this case.
6. **EUDR does not apply.** The cargo went to Egypt [S15]; EUDR covers the Union market [S8]. EU exports were 6.8% of the 2025 total [S6]. No official has tied this case to EUDR.
7. **MSPO already mixes.** MSPO mass balance allows certified and non-certified palm oil in one product (claims down to >30% certified) [S13]. "Undermines MSPO certification" (anonymous industry source [S16a]) overstates what MSPO promises about physical origin, though MSPO is a Malaysian scheme and non-certified foreign oil would still not be MSPO-certified.
8. **Imports have a benign explanation.** MBSB ties the 2025 tripling mainly to oleochemical plants [S22c]; October 2026 CPO imports were zero [S22a].
9. **The "cocktail" is unsourced.** Ratios are "reportedly circulating" among unnamed traders; direction unstated [S18].
10. **Codex is the wrong instrument** for bulk cargo [S14].
11. **Indonesian price gap is not a motive in this case.** The RM700 to RM900 gap is an industry estimate for CPO [S18]; the Indonesian cargo was described as refined product that had already cleared Dumai [S15]. No source shows relabelling at Port Klang would change any duty or levy.
12. **Comparator caution.** Indonesia's customs chief announced lab results and volumes for a misdeclared palm-derivative export at a press conference [S23b, S23c]; different law, different offence (export-duty evasion). Usable only as an example that disclosure is possible, not as a like-for-like.
