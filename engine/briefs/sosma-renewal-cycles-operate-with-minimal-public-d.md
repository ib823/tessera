# BRIEF: SOSMA's five-year renewal under s. 4(11): a vote to extend the 28-day power, not a review of whether it should exist

**Issue ID:** 1298
**Slug:** sosma-renewal-cycles-operate-with-minimal-public-d
**Source date:** 2026-04-08
**Backfill date:** 2026-11-08
**Brief status:** Source-list backfill. Issue cards unchanged; findings below are for the editor.

## ISSUE

Under s. 4(11) of the Security Offences (Special Measures) Act 2012, the s. 4(5) power to hold a suspect for up to 28 days "shall be reviewed every five years". It lapses unless both Houses pass a resolution to extend it [1]. The Dewan Rakyat rejected the extension on 23 March 2022 [6], reversed itself in July and passed it 111–88 on 26 July 2022, running from 31 July 2022 [2, 5]. Civil-society groups (the Malaysian Bar, Suaram, Lawyers for Liberty) urged MPs to reject it or to repeal SOSMA [7, 8, 9]. A separate parliamentary select committee reviewed SOSMA in December 2025, outside the renewal mechanism [13].

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **6,892 period.** FMT reports Suaram's figure as "between 2018 and 2023" [10]. Malaysiakini reports it as "since 2012" [11]. The Home Minister's Dewan Negara written reply gives 6,892 detained from 2012 to 2025 [12]. **Judged authoritative:** the ministerial reply. The figure is official, cited by Suaram.
- **March 2022 vote count.** Malay Mail reports 84 for, 86 against and 50 absent [6]. The Bar statement as reported by The Vibes gives 86 to 84 [7]. Consistent.
- **Omitted context.** The December 2025 select-committee review [13] cuts against the cards' implication that Parliament never examines SOSMA on substance. It is not a contradiction of any single card sentence.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

No material change found. The s. 4(5) extension runs from 31 Jul 2022, so the next s. 4(11) review falls due before 31 Jul 2027 [2]. No SOSMA amendment bill was found tabled by 8 Nov 2026. This was checked by search only.
