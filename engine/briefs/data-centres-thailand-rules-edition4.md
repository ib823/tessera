## ISSUE AND MALAYSIAN MECHANISM

Published issue 2016 asks why Peninsular data centres have a dedicated water-tariff category while the EIA Order has no dedicated data-centre category. Its underlying mechanism is the interaction of prescribed-activity thresholds, planning permission and public participation. A properly sourced Thai comparison could show a different regulatory response to resource reservations, noise, heat or community assurance. It cannot establish that Malaysia offers no regulation or participation.

The new material is currently insufficient to say that Thailand has introduced mandatory EIAs for large data centres, guaranteed public hearings, enacted a law, or implemented the reported framework. One Bangkok Post feed excerpt explicitly says **approved in principle**. Nikkei's saved record contains only a headline and no operative thresholds or legal text. Keep those distinctions visible until the Thai Cabinet resolution and implementing instrument are obtained.

## EXISTING CORRECTIONS AND CONTRADICTIONS TO PRESERVE

1. **Minister and MW correction, edition 2:** `src/data/issues/2016.json` says the 28 October answer was from Energy Transition Minister **Fadillah Yusof**, giving **1,374MW used of 2,346MW contracted in August**. The earlier issue had credited Johari Abdul Ghani with 117 approved centres and 23,000–23,500MW in that answer. The original brief and portions of the saved Stage 3 text still contain the superseded attribution. They are historical review evidence, not a reason to restore it.
2. **Notice-template correction, edition 3:** a council planning-notice reference was removed because its source could not be confirmed. Do not revive the template claim from Stage 3's `omitted_facts` list. Fresh retrieval and checking of applicability would be required, and any new claim must reconcile the published correction explicitly.
3. **No category vs no assessment:** Stage 3 rejected the earlier “only industrial estate” simplification. Qualifying power stations, multi-activity treatment, subdivisions or expansion can matter. Check the legal test rather than inferring exemption from the absence of a campus label.
4. **Legal geography:** Act 172 is not a universal description of all Malaysian planning law; Sabah, Sarawak and Kuala Lumpur require separate treatment. Retain the existing issue's Peninsular/Act 172 scope and qualify it further where needed.
5. **Thai framework vs law:** “approved in principle”, “new regulations” and a headline about “environmental rules” are not interchangeable legal statuses. Obtain the hierarchy and distinguish Cabinet policy, subordinate legislation, permit conditions and commencement.
6. **Public assurance vs public participation:** neither phrase establishes a legal right to notice, comment, hearing, appeal or a veto. Record each procedural right separately in the comparative table once verified.
7. **Applications vs use vs capacity:** AMRO's historical applications, contracted demand, actual consumption, installed supply and eventual project demand have different denominators. This update does not need new MW totals; retain corrected definitions if any remain.
8. **Corporate commitment vs legal duty:** Google's promise to pay campus power costs is its announced commitment, not proof that every operator has the same enforceable obligation or that every cost is covered.
