## ISSUE

Political actors invoke "the social contract" as if it were constitutional text. The cards show that the phrase does
not appear in the Federal Constitution. They show that UMNO anchors it on Article 153 and the Malay Rulers, while DAP
foregrounds Article 8 equality and also says it upholds Article 153. The 1957 Reid Commission Report records negotiated
compromises without calling them a contract or a bargain. The cards treat the "social contract" as a contested narrative
read against the text, not as a fixed provision.

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **Is the 1957 settlement a "social contract" at all?** Tommy Thomas (10) calls it a quid pro quo of 1957 that has since
  lost meaning. Chandra Muzaffar (11) says no such document exists and the term was popularised in the 1980s. Wan Ahmad
  Fauzi et al. (8) reject the negotiated-contract reading and locate authority in the Malay Rulers. UMNO (3, 4) treats it
  as binding principle. This is the contest the cards describe. None of them is "authoritative". The Constitution text
  (1) and the Reid Report (2) are the only fixed points, and neither uses the term.
- **DAP's position across time.** The 2006 statement (5) reads Article 153 narrowly, as help for those in need. The 2020
  statement (7) lists upholding Article 153 without qualification. Both are DAP's own words. The card's "while also
  stating it upholds Article 153" is consistent with the later statement.
