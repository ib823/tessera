# BRIEF: Malaysia's deficit fell to 4.1% of GDP in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025; the PFFRA sets the 3% path

## ISSUE

The federal fiscal deficit was 4.1% of GDP in 2024 against a 4.3% target, and 3.7% in 2025 against an initial 3.8% projection, per Ministry of Finance statements of February 2025 and February 2026. The Public Finance and Fiscal Responsibility Act 2023 (Act 850) sets quantified medium-term values, including a fiscal balance no worse than minus 3% of GDP and debt no higher than 60% of GDP, and requires a fiscal adjustment plan if they are not achieved. The issue argues that one year on target does not establish the glide path; the test is whether the Act's adjustment mechanism is used if the path slips.

## CONTRADICTIONS

1. **2025 deficit.** IMF staff (discussions ended 19 Dec 2025) estimated 3.8% of GDP [8]; MOF's later outturn is 3.7% [2, 4]. MOF's figure is the actual outturn and is authoritative.
2. **2025 debt ratio.** IMF estimated federal debt at 64.8% of GDP for 2025 [8]; Fiscal Outlook 2027 records 65.2% [4]. MOF's later figure is authoritative; both exceed the Act's 60% value.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- **2026 target revised up.** Budget 2027 (9 Oct 2026): the West Asia crisis raised fuel subsidies to RM40 billion and the 2026 deficit "is now projected at 3.6 per cent, compared with the original target of 3.5 per cent" [5]. Fiscal Outlook 2027 projects 3.3% for 2027 and calls the 3.6% revision consistent with Act 850 consolidation [4]. The cards' "glide path" question is now live: 2026 moved off the planned path.
- **Debt above the Act's value.** Federal government debt was 65.2% of GDP at end-2025 and 63.1% at end-June 2026 [4], against the First Schedule's 60% medium-term value [3]. Fiscal Outlook 2027 does not mention a fiscal adjustment plan under s. 26 or s. 27 [4]. This bears directly on the view card.
