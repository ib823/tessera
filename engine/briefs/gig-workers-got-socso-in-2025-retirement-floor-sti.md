# BRIEF: Gig Workers Act 2025 made SOCSO registration mandatory; EPF retirement stayed outside

**Slug:** gig-workers-got-socso-in-2025-retirement-floor-sti

**Backfill date: 2026-11-08**

## ISSUE

The Gig Workers Act 2025 (Act 872), gazetted on 31 December 2025 and in force from 31 March 2026, obliges platform providers to register gig workers under PERKESO's Self-Employment Social Security Scheme and to deduct and remit their contributions. The Act contains no Employees Provident Fund (EPF) retirement contribution and no general paid sick leave; retirement saving for gig workers stays voluntary through EPF i-Saraan. The issue argues that the Act closes the accident-insurance gap, the cheapest one, while the retirement and ordinary-illness gaps remain open.

## CONTRADICTIONS

1. **i-Saraan coverage share, 2022.** World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 10629 (Dec 2023) says i-Saraan "covered 10.7% of all self-employed workers (defined as own account workers and unpaid family workers) in 2022" [7, p. 2]. The World Bank report *Informal Employment in Malaysia* (Feb 2024) says the same membership equals "12.6 percent (year 2022) of all self-employed workers, defined as own account workers and unpaid family members" [6, p. 30]. Dividing 291,743 by DOSM's December 2022 own-account count (2.86 million [4]) gives about 10.2% of own-account workers alone; adding about 0.49 million unpaid family workers gives about 8.7%. No figure is clearly authoritative. Card 0146 does not use a percentage; issue 1128 does (see its brief).
2. **Number of gig workers.** KESUMA said "over 1.2 million" when the Bill passed (Sep 2025, [13]); the Minister cited about 1.64 million when the Act came into force (Mar 2026, [9]); ISIS estimates about 1.6 million, or 3.3 million on a broader own-account definition [8]; the Budget 2027 speech counts about 600,000 e-hailing and p-hailing drivers [5]. Card 0146 uses none of these numbers.
3. **Own-account workers, 2022.** Card cites 2.88 million; DOSM's monthly series gives 2.86 million for December 2022 [4]. The difference is likely annual versus monthly series; the annual figure was not opened.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- **Budget 2027 (9 Oct 2026).** The Gig Workers Consultative Council is negotiating a minimum income rate and "minimum standards of social protection", expected to be finalised in early 2027. Government and Grab will share a RM160 million package from 2027; a 35% SOCSO matching incentive for e-hailing and p-hailing workers (50% if the platform also contributes); and an EPF matching incentive of up to RM600 a year (lifetime cap RM6,000) for e-hailing and p-hailing drivers [5, 15]. EPF saving stays voluntary, so the cards' central claim (EPF retirement outside the Act) still holds. A similar i-Saraan matching incentive had already been announced in Budget 2026 (Oct 2025), before this issue's source date; the cards do not mention it.
- **ISIS Malaysia policy brief (6 Jul 2026)** [8] confirms the 1.25%-per-task deduction and lists further gaps (no compulsory shared social-protection financing, no collective-bargaining pathway, no working-time rules). Consistent with the cards.
