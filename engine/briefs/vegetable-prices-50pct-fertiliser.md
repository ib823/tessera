## ISSUE

In late March 2026, as the festive maximum-price scheme for Hari Raya ended, Malaysian farmers warned that vegetable prices could jump by up to 50% and settle about 30% higher, citing fertiliser and diesel costs driven by the Hormuz disruption. The issue compares Malaysia with Indonesia, Vietnam and Thailand, which have fertiliser stockpiles, price-stabilisation lists or reserve monitoring, and argues that Malaysia lacks equivalent buffers.

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **Who warned of 30-50%.** The cards attribute the April input-cost outlook to the Federation of Vegetable Farmers (correct: secretary Tan Chee Kiong [5]). The 30-50% retail warning itself came from the Cameron Highlands Malay Farmers Association [4, 12], not the Federation. The headline says "farmers", which covers both.
- **Hormuz share of fertiliser trade.** RSIS: one-third of seaborne fertiliser trade [8]. The CodeBlue summary of RSIS says the Gulf is "30 to 35 per cent of global urea exports" and "up to 30 per cent of internationally traded fertilisers" [16]. The RSIS commentary itself is authoritative; the card's "one-third" matches it.
- **Malaysian buffers.** The card says no public-stock mechanism was found; NST reports Nafas buffer stocks [13]. Nafas is the more specific source.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- The Star, 14 Aug 2026: the vegetable farmers' federation president said a 50kg bag of fertiliser cost about RM200, 10% more; vegetable supply was adequate; the situation was "less tense" than March to May. A retailer said prices rose about 15% from March and fell about 5% in July [15]. This suggests the 30-50% warning did not materialise in full at retail, though no DOSM vegetable price series was opened to confirm.
