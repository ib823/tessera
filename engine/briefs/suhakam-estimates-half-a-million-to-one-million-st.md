# BRIEF: Statelessness in Sabah: a 2022 estimate of half a million to one million stateless people, and the much smaller counts of children

**Issue ID:** 1247
**Slug:** suhakam-estimates-half-a-million-to-one-million-st
**Source date:** 2026-04-08
**Backfill date:** 2026-11-08
**Brief status:** Source-list backfill. Issue cards unchanged. **The headline figure is misattributed to children; see CLAIM VERIFICATION and CONTRADICTIONS.**

## ISSUE

In April 2022, SUHAKAM visited Sabah and spoke on statelessness. Malay Mail's report said "no official figure has been reached" and that the numbers "are estimated to be between half a million and one million **people**" [1]. The estimate is for stateless people of all ages, not children. The best-documented child figures are much smaller. UNICEF's 2019 Sabah study estimated 18,781 "invisible" children missing from official databases. It also cited 23,154 undocumented children or young adults in Sabah as of October 2017 [3]. The UN Committee on the Rights of the Child (February 2026) singled out Bajau Laut children for low birth registration and lack of access to citizenship [7].

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **People versus children.** Malay Mail (19 Apr 2022) gives 0.5–1 million stateless **people** [1]. The cards say "children". No source found supports the children framing. **Judged authoritative:** the 2022 report as written. This is the issue's headline number, so it needs a correction or an edition update.
- **Size of the stateless population.** The 2022 estimate is 0.5–1 million [1]. Malay Mail (9 Oct 2024) reports an NGO estimate of 300,000–500,000 local-borns, and an NRD figure of 23,154 stateless under-21s in Sabah [10]. UNICEF cites 23,154 undocumented children or young adults as of October 2017 [3]. The figures measure different populations. None is a census, and none should be presented as one.
- **Name of the 2024 amendment.** The CRC concluding observations call it the "Constitution (Amendment) Act, 2025" [7]. Malaysian reporting and Hansard refer to the Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2024. Likely an enactment-year difference; this issue's cards do not depend on it.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- **25 Jul 2026:** SUHAKAM criticised a Court of Appeal ruling that children born out of wedlock to Malaysian fathers and non-Malaysian mothers are not entitled to citizenship. It urged use of Article 15A to prevent statelessness [11]. This widens the at-risk group the cards describe but does not contradict them.
