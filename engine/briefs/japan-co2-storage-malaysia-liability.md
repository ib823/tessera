## ISSUE

**Verdict on the working thesis: the mechanism holds, three parts of the framing do not.**

*What holds (primary-sourced).*
1. **Inventory rule.** Under the 2006 IPCC Guidelines, when CO2 is captured in Country A and stored in Country B,
   "Country A should report the amount of CO2 captured, any emissions from transport and/or temporary storage that
   takes place in Country A, and the amount of CO2 exported to Country B. Country B should report the amount of CO2
   imported, any emissions from transport and/or temporary storage (that takes place in Country B), and any emissions
   from injection and geological storage sites" [S3a p. 5.20]. Captured CO2 is subtracted at source
   ("Emissions = Production − Capture", Equation 2.7) and "any subsequent emissions from CO2 transport, CO2
   injection and the storage reservoir itself should be counted in national total emissions" [S3b pp. 2.36–2.37].
   The 2019 Refinement made "No Refinement" to Chapter 5 [S3c]. So the capturing country reports the CO2 as captured and exported,
   which lowers its reported emissions; the storing country reports any emissions from injection and the storage
   site (corrected wording per verification item 22; Japan's own inventory treatment unconfirmed, GAP 7). This is
   inventory reporting; it creates no credit.
2. **Federal law passes a closed site's monitoring and repair duties to the Government (s. 32(1)-(3)).** Under Act 870, s. 32(1): "Where a storage site has been
   closed pursuant to section 31, the obligations relating to monitoring, corrective measures and remediation
   measures shall be transferred to the Government and the Government shall issue a certificate to the offshore
   operator to document the transfer of obligations" [S1a p. 24]. Post-transfer costs are paid from a
   Post-Closure Stewardship Fund made up of Government sums, an operator injection levy and investment income
   (s. 41(2)) [S1a pp. 27–28]. The levy "shall not be increased due to withdrawals from the Fund or risks associated
   with other offshore storage projects" (s. 30(2)(b)) [S1a p. 23].
3. **Japan's projects really do target Malaysia.** METI's June 2024 list of nine "Advanced CCS Projects" includes
   three storing CO2 off Malaysia: Northern Offshore of Peninsular Malaysia (about 3 Mtpa), Offshore Sarawak
   (about 1.9–2.9 Mtpa) and Southern Offshore of Peninsular Malaysia (about 5 Mtpa) [S7].
4. **A Malaysian ministry has said it out loud.** FMT reported (22 May 2025) a ministry statement that receiving
   CO2 from Japan "does not affect Malaysia's NDC target" but "if the storage site leaks in the future, then Malaysia
   will be responsible for it" [S21]. **Not opened (fetch returned 404); off cards until opened (GAP 4).**

*What the research changed.*
- **"No fixed minimum waiting period" is wrong as a statement of current law, or at least unsupported.** The Act
  fixes no number, but s. 32(2) lets the transfer occur "only" if "(a) a prescribed period has lapsed from the
  issuance of the closure certificate" and "(b) other prescribed conditions in the closure certificate have been
  fulfilled" [S1a p. 24]. Pinsent Masons' "does not specify the duration" (25 Mar 2025) was written about the Bill
  [S10b]. The Offshore Permit and Licensing Regulations 2025 (P.U.(A) 346/2025, in force 1 Oct 2025) reportedly set
  transfer "after at least 10 years, subject to proof of permanent CO₂ containment" (LH-AG, 16 Oct 2025) [S10a].
  **The regulation text was not opened (GAP 1).** The Economy Minister was reported as saying liability passes
  "the contract for the site is for a certain period – 20 to 30 years – after which [responsibility for the site] will be returned to the government" (Eco-Business, 10 Mar 2025) [S20c]; this is a contract-term statement, not a post-closure period (corrected per verification item 15). The same article reports: "in Malaysia, the government will not bear any costs of CCUS projects, said Rafizi" (reporter's paraphrase), against s. 42(1), which spends the Fund on "costs to be borne by the Government for long-term stewardship of offshore storage sites after the transfer of obligations" [S1a].
- **"Can take over" understates the Act and "obligations" is not "liability".** The transfer is mandatory once the
  prescribed period and conditions are met ("shall be transferred"). And s. 32(3): the transfer "shall not include
  the transfer of any criminal, contractual or civil law liability connected to the storage site attributable to the
  fault, negligence or deceit of the offshore operator, or failure of the offshore operator to exercise any due
  diligence" [S1a p. 24]. What passes to the Government is the no-fault tail: monitoring, corrective and remediation
  duties after transfer.
- **Jurisdiction narrows the thesis.** "This Act shall apply to Peninsular Malaysia and the Federal Territory of
  Labuan" (s. 2) [S1a p. 8]; DPM Fadillah Yusof confirmed it does not cover Sarawak or Sabah [S19d]. Sarawak runs its
  own regime: the Environment (Reduction of Greenhouse Gases Emission) Ordinance 2023 covers "all activities related
  to carbon capture and storage" whether "onshore or offshore Sarawak" and is read with the Land (Carbon Storage)
  Rules, 2022 [S8 pp. 10, 36]. The Offshore Sarawak project's M3 storage site agreement was signed with PETROS; the
  release quotes PETROS's signatory on its role as "the Resource Manager for Carbon Capture, Utilisation and Storage
  (CCUS) in Sarawak" [S12b] (exact sentence to re-check). So the
  federal s. 32 transfer applies to the two Peninsular projects (about 8 Mtpa), not to the Sarawak one. Sarawak's
  closure and transfer rules were not opened (GAP 2). Whether federal continental-shelf law applies off Sarawak is
  itself before the Federal Court, hearing 25–29 Jan 2027 [S22a].
- **Everything Japan-related is still planned, not done.** No CO2 has been shipped. Petronas said on 1 Apr 2026 that
  no agreement had been signed with Japan or Singapore and a bilateral agreement would be needed (reporter's
  paraphrase) [S15]. On 15 Sep 2026, in Seoul, Ministry of Economy officials met representatives of the Governments
  of Japan and Korea "towards the finalisation of bilateral agreements on cross-border CCS" [S9a]. The import-permit regulations were still a draft being finalised in June 2026 [S9b],
  and the AGC lists no Act 870 subsidiary law beyond P.U.(A) 346/2025 and the commencement notice, so no
  injection-levy order is gazetted [S2]. The Southern hub joint venture (signed 23 Oct 2026) has no FID and targets
  first injection "as early as 2031" [S12d].
- **"Critique only abroad" is overstated.** The Star ran "We risk becoming carbon waste dump" (14 Mar 2025) with
  SAM and Klima Action Malaysia raising long-term risk and sovereignty [S20b]; FMT carried SAM's "serious gaps"
  (10 Mar 2025) [S20a] and the ministry's leak-responsibility line (22 May 2025) [S21]. The "carbon colonialism"
  label came from a joint SAM and FoE Japan release (21 Mar 2024) [S18a] and was amplified by Climate Home [S17] and
  AP [S16]. What we did not find in sampled Malaysian coverage: any explanation of the s. 32 transfer combined with
  the IPCC reporting split (GAP 12).

**London Protocol layer (strengthens one side, weakens another).** Japan is a London Protocol party (accession
2 Oct 2007), accepted the 2009 export amendment on 16 Dec 2025 and declared provisional application on
16 Oct 2025; Malaysia is party to neither the Protocol nor the 1972 Convention [S5 pp. 399–404]. Amended Article
6(2) allows export only under "an agreement or arrangement" that includes, for export to non-Contracting Parties
such as Malaysia, "provisions at a minimum equivalent to those contained in this Protocol" [S6a]. The 2009
resolution stresses that "accountability for compliance with the provisions of the Protocol will rest with the
Contracting Party in the case of export to non-Contracting Parties" [S6a]. So Japan cannot lawfully export without
a bilateral instrument, and that instrument (unsigned, unpublished) is the place where long-term duties could be
allocated.

**Developable core (anger at process, not at Japan, Petronas, Sarawak or NGOs).** Malaysia has enacted the transfer
(s. 32) and the fund (s. 41) but not yet the price (the levy order), the import rules or the Japan agreement. The
inventory rule already decides who reports a leak. The documents that decide who pays to watch a closed site, and
for how long, are partly written: the gazetted Regulations reportedly set a minimum of 10 years after closure
(LH-AG and Skrine, Regulation 42(c); text unopened). What is not yet made as at 18 Nov 2026: no injection-levy
order, no import regulations and no public Japan–Malaysia instrument (corrected per verification B3). The Act names
the Government as bearer of post-transfer stewardship costs (s. 42(1)) and bars raising the levy when the Fund is
drawn down (s. 30(2)(b)).

## RELEVANT LAW

- **Carbon Capture, Utilization and Storage Act 2025 (Act 870)** [S1a]; in force 1 Oct 2025 (P.U.(B) 355/2025) [S2]:
  - **s. 1(3) (p. 8):** Part VII (onshore) comes into operation in a State only after consultation with that State.
  - **s. 2 (p. 8):** "This Act shall apply to Peninsular Malaysia and the Federal Territory of Labuan."
  - **s. 4(1) (p. 10):** "offshore area" means "the seabed and subsoil of the seas of Malaysia beyond three nautical
    miles from the baselines of each State, including the exclusive economic zone and the continental shelf";
    s. 4(2) (p. 11) extends references to Malaysia to the EEZ and continental shelf. How s. 2 and s. 4 interact for
    seas off Sarawak and Sabah is not settled in any document opened (GAP 3).
  - **s. 19(1) (p. 18):** "No person shall import for permanent storage any carbon dioxide which is obtained through
    carbon capture outside of Malaysia unless the carbon dioxide complies with the carbon dioxide stream acceptance
    criteria specified in section 28." Offence: fine up to RM1 million or up to five years, or both (s. 19(2)).
  - **s. 20(1) (p. 18):** imports only under "an import permit granted by the Agency"; application "in such manner
    ... as prescribed" (s. 20(2)). Import regulations were in draft in June 2026 [S9b] and are not in the AGC list [S2].
  - **s. 23 (p. 19):** imported CO2 may not be utilised; storage only.
  - **s. 26(1):** offshore storage licence covers CO2 "captured within or outside of Malaysia".
  - **s. 30 (p. 23):** Minister sets injection-levy rates by gazetted order "for the purpose of long-term monitoring
    of each storage site by the Government"; (2)(a) risk-based; (2)(b) not increased for Fund withdrawals or other
    projects' risks; (3)(a) review every five years. No order found [S2].
  - **s. 31 (pp. 23–24):** closure certificate only when "prescribed conditions for closure" met; operator stays
    responsible for monitoring, corrective and remediation measures "until obligations ... [are] transferred to the
    Government pursuant to section 32".
  - **s. 32 (p. 24):** quoted in ISSUE: (1) mandatory transfer with certificate; (2) prescribed period after closure
    certificate plus prescribed conditions; (3) operator keeps fault-based criminal, contractual and civil liability.
  - **s. 40 (p. 27):** onshore transfer "determined by the Minister, after consultation with the State Government".
  - **ss. 41–42 (pp. 27–28):** Fund = Government sums + injection levy + investment income; spent "only" on
    Government's post-transfer stewardship costs, except that investment income "may also be expended for the purpose
    of promoting the development of the carbon capture, utilization and storage industry in Malaysia generally"
    (s. 42(2)).
  - **s. 44:** audited Fund accounts laid before each House of Parliament.
  - **s. 49 (p. 31):** no suit against the Minister, Agency members or officers unless bad faith and without
    reasonable cause.
  - **s. 51 (p. 32):** Minister may by gazetted order exempt "any geographical area, person or class of person".
  - **s. 52(2)(i), (j):** regulations prescribe closure conditions and transfer conditions.
- **CCUS (Offshore Permit and Licensing) Regulations 2025, P.U.(A) 346/2025** [S2]: text **not opened** (GAP 1).
  Reported content: Part VII operating, closure and post-closure duties; financial security for "closure and
  post-closure costs"; transfer "after at least 10 years, subject to proof of permanent CO₂ containment" [S10a]; fee
  RM120,000 for initiating storage-site closure (as reported) [S10d].
- **Sarawak:** Environment (Reduction of Greenhouse Gases Emission) Ordinance, 2023 (Cap. 85) [S8]: s. 2 defines
  "atmosphere" to include airspace above "waters above the continental shelf off the coasts of Sarawak" (p. 6);
  s. 3(1)(b) covers capture and storage "in any facility on land whether onshore or offshore of Sarawak"; s. 3(3)
  read with the Land (Carbon Storage) Rules, 2022 [Swk. L.N. 349/2022] (p. 10); First Schedule A: "all activities
  related to carbon capture and storage" (p. 36). Commencement "on such dates as the Minister may ... appoint"
  (commencement status not checked, GAP 2). The Land (Carbon Storage) Rules 2022 were **not opened** (GAP 2).
- **Sabah:** Wong & Partners (Apr 2025) said Sabah "has yet to pass state legislation to govern CCUS activities" [S10e]; superseded: Sabah Land Ordinance (Cap. 68) s. 23B now requires the Minister's approval and a Collector's licence for "carbon storage", and Sabah passed a Climate Change and Carbon Governance Enactment on 8 Jul 2025 (verification item 46). Off cards.
- **London Protocol 1996, Article 6 as amended by LP.3(4)** [S6a]: export allowed under "an agreement or arrangement"
  covering "confirmation and allocation of permitting responsibilities" and, for non-Parties, "provisions at a minimum
  equivalent to those contained in this Protocol"; the Party must notify the IMO. Not in force (17 of 56 acceptances;
  two-thirds needed); provisional application by declaration under LP.5(14) [S5, S6b].
- **IPCC 2006 Guidelines, Vol 2 Ch 5 s. 5.10 and Ch 2 s. 2.3.4** [S3a, S3b]: reporting split quoted in ISSUE. These are
  inventory rules, not liability rules.
- **Japan, Act on Carbon Dioxide Storage Business (Act No. 38 of 2024)** (comparator; text not opened): per Clifford
  Chance, the operator "is also liable for any damage caused by CO2 leakage on a strict liability basis ... even after
  closure of the storage site and its transfer to JOGMEC" [S11]. This governs storage in Japan, not in Malaysia.

## WHAT WEAKENS THE THESIS

1. **Nothing has crossed the sea.** No CO2 shipped; no bilateral instrument signed as of the last official update
   (15 Sep 2026) [S9a, S15]; Japan cannot lawfully export to Malaysia without one [S6a]. The issue is about rules
   being set, not harm done.
2. **The handover is not immediate.** A prescribed period after the closure certificate and prescribed conditions
   must both be met (s. 32(2)); reportedly at least 10 years plus proof of containment [S1a, S10a].
3. **Operators keep fault liability.** s. 32(3) keeps criminal, contractual and civil liability for fault,
   negligence, deceit or lack of due diligence with the operator [S1a p. 24].
4. **Operators pay into the Fund.** The levy is risk-based and paid by operators (s. 30) [S1a p. 23]; operators must
   also post financial security for "closure and post-closure costs" (reported) [S10a].
5. **The federal handover does not reach Sarawak.** The Offshore Sarawak project and, likely, Shepherd sit under
   Sarawak's regime; its rules on closure and transfer are unopened and may differ [S8, S12b].
6. **The Protocol puts duties on Japan.** For exports to non-Parties, the arrangement must hold provisions "at a
   minimum equivalent" to the Protocol, and accountability for compliance "will rest with the Contracting Party"
   [S6a]. The negotiated instrument could allocate post-closure duties or costs to the exporter.
7. **Storing countries taking long-term responsibility is normal.** The IPCC noted in 2005 that "some States take on
   long-term responsibility in situations comparable to CO2 storage, such as underground mining operations" [S4 p.
   15]; Japan's own law moves storage rights to JOGMEC after closure [S11].
8. **Leak risk is low on the evidence.** Retention "very likely to exceed 99% over 100 years" for well-selected sites
   [S4 p. 14]. The long tail is small in probability, long in time.
9. **Malaysia is paid.** AP reports Malaysia "will likely be paid an undetermined amount per ton" [S16]; the
   government expects industry and jobs (promotional figures) [S9d].
10. **Malaysian media did report the critique.** The Star, FMT and the government itself addressed dumping and
    leak responsibility [S20a, S20b, S21]. The gap is narrower: the s. 32 plus IPCC combination was not explained.
11. **The accounting rule may change.** The IPCC's 2027 Methodology Report on CDR and CCUS is in preparation and
    covers transport, including cross-border issues (scope from search index; GAP 13) [S3c].

### Misleading-framing risks

1. **"Hands closed sites to the Government"** (brief title, ISSUE item 2 heading, draft hook). The Act transfers
   "obligations relating to monitoring, corrective measures and remediation measures", not the site, and only after a
   prescribed period and prescribed conditions; fault-based liability stays with the operator.
2. **"About 10 million tonnes" read as Japanese CO2.** The METI figures are 2024 planned capacities for projects at
   study or design stage. The Southern hub's 5 Mtpa will take domestic and regional CO2. The Northern project was not
   yet under contract when selected. Cards must say "planned (METI, 2024)", never "Japanese CO2 Malaysia will hold".
3. **"None is public" / "for how long ... being written now".** P.U.(A) 346/2025 is gazetted and public, and (per two
   law firms) already fixes a 10-year minimum and the transfer conditions. What is not made or not public: the levy
   order, the import regulations and the Japan–Malaysia instrument.
4. **"The levy has no rate yet" as the arousal peak.** True, but no storage licence or injection exists under Act 870
   yet (one assessment permit, Duyong). A missing rate before any injection is ordinary sequencing, not a lapse. The
   stronger, sourced tension: s. 30(2)(b) bars raising the levy for Fund withdrawals, s. 42(1) says stewardship costs
   are "borne by the Government", and the Minister was reported saying "the government will not bear any costs of
   CCUS projects".
5. **IPCC split as "Japan books the reduction, Malaysia books the leak".** Inventory reporting only, not credits;
   Japan's practice unconfirmed; Malaysia also reports injection emissions and the imported amount.
6. **London Protocol as a liability lever.** Art. 6(2) requires allocation of *permitting* responsibilities and
