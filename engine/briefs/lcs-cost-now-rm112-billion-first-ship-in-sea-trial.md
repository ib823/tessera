## ISSUE

The Littoral Combat Ship (LCS) programme was signed with Boustead Naval Shipyard (BNS) in July 2014 at RM9.128 billion for six ships, with the first ship due in April 2019. A sixth supplemental contract in May 2023 cut the order to five ships and raised the contract value to about RM11.2 billion (RM11.22 billion in later ministerial replies). The first ship, Maharaja Lela, made its first sea-going on 28 January 2026; at this issue's source date its delivery was scheduled for December 2026. Since publication the delivery has slipped again, to April 2027.

Sister issue: 0154 covers the same programme from the 2022 PAC report (brief `lcs-rm9b-ships-pac`). The two issues conflict on the contract-signing year and on how much has been spent; see CONTRADICTIONS.

## PERIOD

December 2011 (letter of award) to October 2026 (delivery moved to April 2027).

## CONTEXT (timeline with sources)

- 16 Dec 2011: letter of award to BNS; final contract signed 17 Jul 2014 at RM9 billion, per the forensic audit as reported by The Sun [12].
- 1 Oct 2013: MINDEF confirms a RM9 billion contract value in an amended letter of acceptance; contract period stated as 29 Dec 2011 to 28 Dec 2021 [13].
- Contract value later stated as RM9.128 billion; agreed delivery of the six ships "between April 2019 and August 2023" (declassified governance-committee report, as reported by Malay Mail) [11].
- 4 Aug 2022: PAC tables its LCS report: RM6.083 billion paid, no ship completed, RM1.4 billion used for other purposes including cost overruns [7][8].
- 11 Aug 2022: MACC completes investigation papers on several individuals and sends them to the AGC [9].
- 16 Aug 2022: former navy chief and former BNS managing director Ahmad Ramli Mohd Nor charged with three counts of CBT (s.409 Penal Code) involving RM21.08 million [19].
- 26 May 2023: sixth supplemental contract signed; five ships instead of six; contract price revised to RM11.2 billion; first ship due August 2026, fifth in April 2029; payment terms moved from milestone activity to progress of works [14].
- Jan 2024: Ocean Sunshine Bhd (a wholly owned MOF Inc subsidiary) holds 79.23% of the shipyard and is buying the rest for RM1; company renamed Lumut Naval Shipyard (LUNAS) [15][6].
- 24 Jun 2024: Defence Minister tells the Dewan Rakyat RM7.15 billion has been paid, 63.69% of the RM11.22 billion total; original budget RM6 billion, later RM9.18 billion, then RM11.22 billion [16][17].
- 12 Mar 2025: Sessions Court grants Ahmad Ramli a discharge not amounting to acquittal (DNAA) after a hospital report found him unfit to stand trial [19].
- 20 Jan 2026: LCS 1 harbour trials begin; 28 Jan 2026 first sea-going; delivery now December 2026, about four months late; about RM8.3 billion (74% of contract value) spent [1][5].
- 19 May 2026: MINDEF says LCS 1 is in sea trials ahead of sea acceptance trials, delivery December 2026 [2].
- 5 to 8 Oct 2026: Auditor-General's Report 2/2026 warns LCS 1 may be handed over without full capability; Defence Minister says delivery moves to April 2027 and a liquidated damages notice has been issued to LUNAS [20][21].

## CONTRADICTIONS

1. **Original contract value.** RM9 billion fixed in the 2013 amended letter of acceptance [13]; RM9.128 billion in the declassified report [11]; "RM9.18 billion" in the minister's 2024 reply as reported by The Edge [16]; "RM9.1 billion" in some 2023 coverage. Authoritative: RM9.128 billion, the figure the PAC and the declassified report used. The Edge's RM9.18 billion is most likely a transcription slip.
2. **Revised contract value.** RM11.2 billion (2023 filing [14]; MINDEF Jan 2026 [1]), RM11.22 billion (Jan 2024 [15], Jun 2024 [16]), RM11.227 billion (Oct 2026 [22]). Rounding of one figure; RM11.227 billion is the most precise.
3. **When the contract was "signed".** Letter of award 16 Dec 2011, final contract 17 Jul 2014 [12]; MINDEF's 2013 letter dates the contract period from 29 Dec 2011 [13]; the declassified report gives a 10-year period from October 2013 [11]. Issue 1805 says July 2014 (supported). Issue 0154 says "contract signed 2011" (contradicted: 2011 was the award, not the signing).
4. **Amount spent.** RM5.936 billion at 31 May 2019 [11]; RM6.083 billion at October 2021 (PAC, 2022) [8]; RM7.15 billion at June 2024 [16]; about RM8.3 billion at January 2026 [1]; RM8.96 billion claimed at 25 Sep 2026 [22]. Issue 1805 does not state a spend figure. Issue 0154's headline "RM6.08B spent" uses the 2022 figure although MINDEF gave RM8.3 billion on 28 Jan 2026, before 0154's source date (17 May 2026).
5. **First-ship delivery date.** April 2019 (original window [11]); August 2026 (2023 contract [14], restated March 2025 [18]); December 2026 (Jan and May 2026 [1][2]); April 2027 (Oct 2026 [21][22]).
6. **DNAA disclosure across issues.** 1805 discloses the March 2025 DNAA; 0154's fact and view cards cite "MACC charges" without it.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- **Delivery slipped to April 2027.** On 8 Oct 2026 Defence Minister Mohamed Khaled Nordin, winding up debate on the Auditor-General's Report 2/2026, said LUNAS has set April 2027 as the new LCS 1 delivery date and MINDEF has issued a liquidated ascertained damages notice [21]. A written reply put overall progress at 80.72% against 89.91% planned (25 Sep 2026), the contract value at RM11.227 billion and LUNAS's claims at RM8.96 billion, 79.82% of contract value [22]. This makes the context and hook "first delivery is scheduled for December 2026" and "about 7 years 8 months past" outdated.
- **Auditor-General's Report 2/2026.** LCS 1 may be handed over without full capability; full readiness depends on complementary contracts that can only finish in December 2035 [20].
