# BRIEF: Article 15A citizenship registration: discretion without published criteria, and multi-year waits

**Issue ID:** 1265
**Slug:** article-15a-citizenship-applications-are-decided-w
**Source date:** 2026-04-08
**Backfill date:** 2026-11-08
**Brief status:** Source-list backfill. Issue cards unchanged; findings below are for the editor.

## ISSUE

Article 15A lets the Federal Government, "in such special circumstances as it thinks fit", register any person under 21 as a citizen [1]. The National Registration Department (JPN) publishes the conditions, documents and fees, but nothing defines "special circumstances" or how applications are decided [2]. In October 2024 the Home Minister told the Dewan Rakyat that 80% of the citizenship approvals he had signed were for applications more than five years old. He also promised decisions on Article 15A and 19(1) applications within one year under a revised SOP [3]. The 2024 constitutional amendment, passed but not in force as of October 2026 [10], lowers Article 15A's age ceiling from 21 to 18 [3, 4].

## CONTRADICTIONS

- **"11-year" case.** The cards attribute an 11-year case to SUHAKAM. The only matching case found is a 2019 Malay Mail report [9] that does not involve SUHAKAM and says "more than a decade". **Judged authoritative:** the Malay Mail report as written. The SUHAKAM attribution is unsupported.
- **Name of the amendment.** The CRC concluding observations call it the "Constitution (Amendment) Act, 2025" [4]. Hansard and Malaysian reporting refer to the Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2024 [3, 8]. Probably a year-of-enactment difference; the cards' "2024 Bill" is accurate for passage.

## SINCE PUBLICATION

- **25 Jul 2026:** SUHAKAM urged the government to keep using Article 15A for children left without citizenship after a Court of Appeal ruling on children born out of wedlock [7].
- **14 Oct 2026:** The Home Minister said the government was still waiting for an enforcement date for the constitutional amendment [10]. Until it commences, the Art. 15A age limit remains 21, as the JPN page states [2].
