## ISSUE

Malaysia publicly supports The Gambia's genocide case against Myanmar at the International Court of Justice, has funded it through the OIC, and told the UN in 2025 that Myanmar's 1982 Citizenship Law "has systematically denied the Rohingya people their fundamental rights". At home, Malaysia is not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention, its Immigration Act 1959/63 has no category for refugees, and the Immigration Department said on 31 Jul 2026 that UNHCR card holders are "still regarded as illegal immigrants". In 2026 Malaysia began its own refugee registration, granted refugee status to 78 Rohingya by July, told UNHCR to stop registering, and agreed with Myanmar on the return of 5,000 Rohingya. The judgment on 19 Nov is the news hook; the Malaysian legal position is the gap mainstream coverage has not connected.

## RELEVANT LAW

- **1951 Refugee Convention and 1967 Protocol:** Malaysia is not a party (UNTC status pages "as at 10-11-2026") [8a][8b].
- **Immigration Act 1959/63 (Act 155)** [9]: s.6(1)(c) no entry without a valid pass; s.6(3) penalty "a fine not exceeding ten thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to both, and shall also be liable to whipping of not more than six strokes"; s.15(1)(c) no remaining after a pass expires; s.55 Minister may exempt "any person or class of persons" by order, gazetted for a class; s.55B employing a person without a valid pass. No occurrence of "refugee" or "asylum" in the text. 2006 reprint; Dec 2025 amendment not yet checked [9b].
- **AG's Chambers circular (2005):** UNHCR says it "establishes that those persons registered with UNHCR at the time of arrest should not be prosecuted for immigration offences" and "is not systematically implemented" [10]. Text not public.
- **NSC Directive No. 23 (revised 2023):** governs refugee management; confidential [13a][13b][11b].
- **Societies Act 1966 (Act 335; revised edition Act 832):** unregistered societies are unlawful; RoS has never registered a Rohingya NGO [12][20].
- **Genocide Convention, Art. II;** ICJ Statute (judgment final, no appeal) [1][2][3].
- **ASEAN Charter Art. 20(1):** "decision-making in ASEAN shall be based on consultation and consensus" [21b].

## WHAT WEAKENS THE THESIS

- Malaysia hosts about 129,400 Rohingya and is building its own registration; 78 granted refugee status and 25 cleared for work within months of starting, with a 2029 completion target [14a][14b].
- Card holders are, per the Immigration Department, exempt from prosecution for entry and overstay, and the DG said they cannot be detained [11a][11c]; a 2005 AG circular points the same way [10].
- Supporting accountability for alleged genocide and managing irregular migration are different legal questions. A card that equates them commits misleading framing.
- Malaysia argues that low UNHCR resettlement prolongs the Rohingya presence [19e] and calls for "burden and responsibility sharing" [6a][5].
- The PM says no Rohingya were among the first returns [19b].
- MEF supports formal work for refugees [24]; the pilot exists.
